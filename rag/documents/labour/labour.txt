[SECTION: MINIMUM_WAGES]
Minimum Wages in Pakistan
- Each province sets its own minimum wage through Minimum Wages Ordinance/Act.
- The Federal Government sets minimum wage for Islamabad Capital Territory.
- Minimum wage applies to all workers in industrial and commercial establishments.
- Employers paying below minimum wage commit an offence punishable by fine.
- Workers can file complaints with the Labour Inspector or Labour Court.
Source: Provincial Minimum Wages Ordinances/Acts; Ministry of Overseas Pakistanis & HRD.

[SECTION: EMPLOYMENT_CONTRACTS]
Employment Contracts
- Employment contracts may be oral or written, but written contracts are strongly recommended.
- Standing Orders Ordinance, 1968 requires establishments with 20+ workers to have written standing orders.
- Key terms: wages, working hours, leave, termination notice, gratuity.
- Probation period is typically 3-6 months.
- Permanent employees cannot be terminated without due process.
Source: Standing Orders Ordinance, 1968; Industrial Relations Act, 2012.

[SECTION: WRONGFUL_TERMINATION]
Wrongful Termination
- An employer must give proper notice (typically one month or as per contract) before terminating a permanent employee.
- Termination without cause or without proper notice is wrongful termination.
- Worker can file a grievance petition before the Labour Court within 30 days.
- Labour Court may order reinstatement and/or back wages.
- Retrenchment (due to business closure) requires 30 days notice and payment of retrenchment benefits.
Source: Industrial and Commercial Employment (Standing Orders) Ordinance, 1968; Industrial Relations Act, 2012.

[SECTION: WORKPLACE_HARASSMENT]
Workplace Harassment
- The Protection against Harassment of Women at the Workplace Act, 2010 applies to all workplaces.
- Every organization must constitute an Inquiry Committee to investigate harassment complaints.
- Harassment includes unwelcome sexual advances, requests for sexual favours, and verbal or physical conduct of sexual nature.
- Complaint must be filed in writing with the Inquiry Committee.
- If organization fails to constitute committee, complaint may be made to the Ombudsperson.
- Federal and Provincial Ombudspersons for harassment are established.
- Penalty includes minor to major penalties (censure, transfer, compulsory retirement, dismissal).
Source: Protection against Harassment of Women at the Workplace Act, 2010.

[SECTION: EOBI]
Employees Old-Age Benefits Institution (EOBI)
- EOBI provides pensions to retired workers.
- Applies to establishments with 5 or more employees.
- Employer contributes 5% of minimum wage per employee per month.
- Employee contributes 1% of minimum wage per month.
- Pension is payable after retirement at age 55 (women) or 60 (men) with minimum 15 years of insurable employment.
- Old-Age Pension, Invalidity Pension, and Survivors Pension are available.
Source: Employees' Old-Age Benefits Act, 1976.

[SECTION: SOCIAL_SECURITY]
Social Security (PESSI/SESSI)
- Provincial Social Security Institutions (PESSI in Punjab, SESSI in Sindh, etc.) provide medical and financial benefits.
- Applies to workers earning below a specified wage ceiling.
- Benefits include medical treatment, sickness benefit, maternity benefit, injury benefit, death grant.
- Employer registers workers and pays contribution.
Source: Provincial Employees Social Security Ordinance, 1965.

[SECTION: WORKING_HOURS_LEAVE]
Working Hours and Leave
- Shops and Establishments Ordinance: Maximum 48 hours per week, 8 hours per day.
- Factories Act, 1934: Maximum 48 hours per week for factory workers.
- Weekly holiday: One day per week (usually Friday or Sunday).
- Annual leave: 14 days per year after completing 12 months of service.
- Sick leave: As per establishment policy or standing orders.
- Casual leave: Typically 10 days per year.
- Maternity leave: 12 weeks (6 weeks before and 6 weeks after delivery) with full pay under applicable laws.
Source: Factories Act, 1934; Shops and Establishments Ordinance (various provinces).

[SECTION: LABOUR_COURT_PROCEDURE]
Labour Court Procedure
1. Filing Grievance: Worker files grievance petition in the Labour Court.
2. Notice: Court issues notice to employer.
3. Written Reply: Employer submits written reply.
4. Conciliation: Labour Officer may attempt conciliation.
5. Evidence: Both parties present evidence.
6. Arguments: Final arguments.
7. Award: Labour Court announces award/decision.
8. Appeal: Appeal lies to the Labour Appellate Tribunal within 30 days.
9. Enforcement: Award can be enforced as a decree of civil court.
Source: Industrial Relations Act, 2012; Punjab Industrial Relations Act, 2010.
