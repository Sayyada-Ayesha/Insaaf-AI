[SECTION: 1] Prisons Act, 1894 and Prison Rules — Regulates administration of prisons in Pakistan. Source: Pakistan Code / Provincial Prison Departments. Status: CURRENT (provincial rules may vary).

[SECTION: 2] Classification of prisoners — Prisoners classified as: (a) convicted criminal prisoners; (b) under-trial prisoners; (c) civil prisoners; (d) female prisoners; (e) juvenile prisoners. Each class must be kept separate.

[SECTION: 3] Rights of prisoners — (a) Right to food, clothing, medical care; (b) right to communication with family and lawyers; (c) right to exercise and recreation; (d) right to religious instruction; (e) right to legal aid; (f) right to be free from torture.

[SECTION: 4] Remission system — Convicted prisoners may earn remission (reduction in sentence) through good conduct: up to 6 days per month for ordinary prisoners, up to 10 days for skilled work. Special remission for literacy, vocational training.

[SECTION: 5] Premature release — Provincial governments may order premature release of prisoners meeting criteria: (a) completed 2/3 of sentence; (b) good conduct; (c) no longer a threat to society. Death sentence cases, certain terrorism offences excluded.

[SECTION: 6] Under-trial prisoners — Under-trial prisoners must be produced before a magistrate within 24 hours. Right to bail. If trial not completed within prescribed period, under-trial may be released on personal bond.

[SECTION: 7] Grievance mechanism — Prisoners may submit petitions to: Superintendent, Inspector General of Prisons, District and Sessions Judge, High Court. Weekly courts of District and Sessions Judge visit prisons.

[SECTION: 8] Overcrowding — Prison overcrowding is a persistent issue. Supreme Court has issued directions to reduce overcrowding, expedite trials, and release under-trials on bail where appropriate.
