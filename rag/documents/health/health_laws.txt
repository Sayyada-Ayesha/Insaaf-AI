# Health Law of Pakistan — Regulators, Facilities, Practitioners, Food and Drugs
# Coverage: DRAP drug regulation, healthcare establishment regulation, medical/dental
# practitioner regulation, food safety authorities, mental health framework. Sources: DRAP Act
# 2012; PMDC Act 2022 (replacing PMDC Ordinance 1962 regime); Islamabad Healthcare Regulation
# Act 2018; provincial food authority acts; provincial mental health acts.

[SECTION: HEALTH_READING_NOTE]
Health regulation in Pakistan is split: the Drug Regulatory Authority of Pakistan (DRAP) regulates
drugs, medical devices and health products federally; healthcare practitioners are regulated by
councils (PM&DC for medical/dental practitioners; provincial nursing and allied-health councils);
healthcare establishments (hospitals, clinics, labs) are regulated provincially (e.g., Islamabad
Healthcare Regulation Act 2018 in ICT, Punjab Healthcare Commission Act 2010 in Punjab, Sindh
Healthcare Commission Act 2013 in Sindh, KP Healthcare Commission Act 2015); food safety is
provincial (food authorities). Complaints route to the relevant regulator, not a single body.

[SECTION: HEALTH_DRAP_DRUGS]
LAW: Drug Regulatory Authority of Pakistan Act, 2012. COVERAGE: PARTIAL.
KEY PROVISIONS (summary of the operative framework):
- DRAP regulates: registration/marketing authorization of drugs and medical devices, licensing
  of drug manufacturing and sales premises, clinical trial oversight, pharmacovigilance,
  quality control, and enforcement against spurious, adulterated and unregistered drugs.
- The Drugs Act, 1976 continues to provide substantive offences (spurious/adulterated drugs,
  unlicensed sale) and penalties.
- Complaints about unsafe or unregistered medicines/devices: DRAP complaint channels (official
  DRAP website/helplines) and provincial drug inspectors.
SOURCE: DRAP Act, 2012; Drugs Act, 1976. VERIFICATION: verified as to framework.

[SECTION: HEALTH_HEALTHCARE_REGULATION]
LAWS: Islamabad Healthcare Regulation Act, 2018 (ICT); Punjab Healthcare Commission Act, 2010;
Sindh Healthcare Commission Act, 2013; Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Healthcare Commission Act, 2015.
COVERAGE: SUMMARY-PARTIAL.
KEY PROVISIONS (summary): each commission licenses and regulates healthcare establishments
(hospitals, clinics, diagnostic labs) in its territory; sets minimum service delivery standards;
receives and investigates complaints of medical negligence/malpractice against registered
establishments; may suspend/cancel licences and impose penalties.
SOURCE: the respective commission acts. VERIFICATION: verified as to the acts' existence and
purpose; complaint procedures should be checked on each commission's official website.

[SECTION: HEALTH_PRACTITIONER_REGULATION]
LAW: Pakistan Medical and Dental Council regime (PMDC Act, 2022, following the PMDC Ordinance
1962 and its successors) and provincial nursing councils. COVERAGE: SUMMARY.
KEY PROVISIONS (summary): PM&DC registers medical and dental practitioners, regulates medical
and dental education and colleges, and disciplines professional misconduct; practising without
registration is an offence. Complaints against practitioners go to PM&DC (or the provincial
council for nursing/allied professionals) in addition to the healthcare commission route for
establishment-level negligence.
SOURCE: PMDC Act, 2022. VERIFICATION: metadata-verified (PMDC's governing statute has been
re-enacted; check the current act).

[SECTION: HEALTH_FOOD_SAFETY]
LAWS: Punjab Food Authority Act, 2011 (Punjab Food Authority); Sindh Food Authority Act, 2016;
KP Food Safety and Halal Food Authority Act, 2014; Balochistan Food Authority Act (verify);
food-safety enforcement in ICT. COVERAGE: SUMMARY.
KEY PROVISIONS (summary): provincial food authorities license and inspect food businesses,
enforce hygiene and labelling standards, act against adulteration (also punishable under the
Pakistan Penal Code sections on food/drink adulteration), and receive consumer complaints.
SOURCE: provincial food authority acts. VERIFICATION: metadata-verified (acts exist; current
authority names and procedures should be checked per province).

[SECTION: HEALTH_MENTAL_HEALTH]
LAWS: Mental Health Ordinance, 2001 (federal, largely superseded provincially) and provincial
mental health acts (e.g., Punjab Mental Health Act, 2020; Sindh Mental Health Act, 2013; KP
Mental Health Act, 2017 — verify titles). COVERAGE: SUMMARY.
KEY PROVISIONS (summary): provide for assessment, voluntary and involuntary admission safeguards,
treatment standards, and rights of persons with mental health conditions; involuntary admission
requires prescribed procedures and safeguards.
SOURCE: Mental Health Ordinance 2001 and provincial acts. VERIFICATION: metadata-verified.

[SECTION: HEALTH_LIMITS]
LIMITS: exact licensing fees, application forms and current complaint portals change; answer
with the framework and point to the relevant authority's official channels. Medical advice is
out of scope for legal guidance — route treatment questions to qualified health professionals.
