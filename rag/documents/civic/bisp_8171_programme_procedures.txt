"Beneficiaries of \"Benazir Kafaalat Programme\" are identified/selected through scientific mode of Proxy Means Test (PMT) through National Socio Economic Registry (NSER) survey."
"The current PMT cut-off score of 32 was approved by the BISP Board in its 52 nd meeting held on 23 rd September, 2021. However, for families with a differently abled person, the PMT cut-off score is set at 37 for eligibility."
Read the two numbers correctly: the cut-off is a ceiling on the household's proxy-means score, and a household containing a differently abled person is assessed against the more generous 37 threshold rather than 32.
Spelling note: BISP publishes this programme name as "Benazir Kafaalat" on its programme pages and as "BENAZIR Kafaalat" / "BISP Kifalat Programme" in its own registration leaflets, and it is commonly written "Benazir Kafalat". Consequence for a citizen: eligibility is decided by the NSER/PMT household record, not by an application form alone. Where the record is stale, the route is a roster update or a new survey, described in the NSER registration record in this file.

Read the bands as level gates, not as a single cut-off: the published ranges overlap by design (8-12 appears in both Primary and Secondary, 13-18 in both Secondary and Higher Secondary), so the child's enrolled level, not age alone, decides which band applies. The admission precondition is equally important: a child who is not admitted in a school or college cannot be enrolled, and the admission slip must show the class and school name verified by the teacher — the grant follows an actual school roll entry. The band limits also mean a child aged 22 or above cannot be enrolled under Higher Secondary, and a child under 4 cannot be enrolled under Primary, on this published wording.
