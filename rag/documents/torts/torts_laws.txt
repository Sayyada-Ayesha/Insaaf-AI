# Law of Torts in Pakistan
# Coverage: defamation, negligence, nuisance, trespass, malicious prosecution, damages and
# limitation. Tort law in Pakistan is largely uncodified common law (except defamation under
# the Defamation Ordinance 2002); principles below reflect the settled position applied by
# Pakistani courts. Sources: Defamation Ordinance, 2002; common-law principles as applied by
# Pakistani courts; Limitation Act, 1908.

[SECTION: TORTS_READING_NOTE]
A tort is a civil wrong (not a breach of contract or trust) for which the law provides damages.
Pakistani courts apply the common-law tort framework as modified by statute (defamation) and by
the Constitution (fundamental rights can support claims against the State). Tort claims are
brought in civil courts; criminal remedies (e.g., criminal defamation under the PPC) are
separate.

[SECTION: TORTS_DEFAMATION]
LAW: Defamation Ordinance, 2002 (civil defamation); Pakistan Penal Code, Section 499-501
(criminal defamation). COVERAGE: PARTIAL.
KEY PROVISIONS (summary):
- Defamation is publication of a false statement that injures a person's reputation.
- Civil: under the Defamation Ordinance 2002, an aggrieved person may sue for damages; defences
  include truth (justification), fair comment on a matter of public interest, and privilege
  (absolute for judicial/parliamentary proceedings; qualified for fair and accurate reports).
- Criminal: PPC Section 499 defines defamation; Section 500 prescribes punishment (imprisonment
  up to two years or fine or both — check the current text for the exact range); defences in
  Section 499's explanations (truth for public good, fair comment, etc.).
- Defamation through electronic systems is also punishable under PECA 2016 (online defamation).
SOURCE: Defamation Ordinance, 2002; PPC ss.499-501; PECA 2016. VERIFICATION: verified as to the
framework (exact penalty text should be checked against the current statute).

[SECTION: TORTS_NEGLIGENCE]
LAW: common-law negligence as applied in Pakistan. COVERAGE: PARTIAL.
ELEMENTS (summary of the settled test):
- Duty of care: the defendant owed the plaintiff a duty to take reasonable care (the
  neighbour principle — persons so closely affected by one's acts that one ought reasonably to
  have them in contemplation).
- Breach: the defendant's conduct fell below the standard of a reasonable person in the
  circumstances (professional negligence judged by professional standards).
- Causation: the breach caused the plaintiff's loss (cause-in-fact and proximate cause).
- Damage: the plaintiff suffered recoverable loss (personal injury, property damage, economic
  loss in recognized cases).
- Medical negligence: judged by the standard of the ordinary skilled practitioner of the
  relevant specialty (Bolam-type standard as applied in Pakistan), and also actionable before
  healthcare commissions as establishment-level negligence.
SOURCE: settled case law. VERIFICATION: verified as settled principles.

[SECTION: TORTS_NUISANCE_TRESPASS]
LAW: common law. COVERAGE: SUMMARY.
- Private nuisance: substantial and unreasonable interference with a person's use or enjoyment
  of land (noise, fumes, encroaching structures, blocked passages); remedies: injunction
  and/or damages. Public nuisance affects the public at large and is also a PPC offence
  (Section 268).
- Trespass to land: unauthorized entry on another's land; trespass to goods: unauthorized
  interference with goods. Remedies: damages, injunction, recovery of possession.
SOURCE: common law; PPC s.268. VERIFICATION: verified as settled principles.

[SECTION: TORTS_MALICIOUS_PROSECUTION]
LAW: common law. COVERAGE: SUMMARY.
ELEMENTS (summary): the defendant prosecuted the plaintiff without reasonable and probable
cause, actuated by malice, and the prosecution ended in the plaintiff's favour (acquittal or
discharge), causing damage. Applies to criminal proceedings; suits for malicious prosecution
must plead and prove these elements.
SOURCE: settled case law. VERIFICATION: verified as settled principles.

[SECTION: TORTS_DAMAGES_LIMITATION]
LAW: common-law damages; Limitation Act, 1908. COVERAGE: SUMMARY.
- Damages: compensatory (to put the plaintiff in the position before the wrong) and, in
  exceptional cases, exemplary/punitive damages; aggravated damages for injury to feelings in
  defamation.
- Limitation: tort suits (defamation, negligence etc.) are barred after the limitation period
  applicable to the wrong under the Limitation Act, 1908 (defamation suits are subject to a
  short limitation period — check the current Schedule); delay can defeat an otherwise valid
  claim.
SOURCE: Limitation Act, 1908. VERIFICATION: verified as to the general framework (exact periods
for each tort should be checked against the Schedule).
