[SECTION: CASE_BENAZIR_BHUTTO_V_FEDERATION]
Case: Benazir Bhutto v. Federation of Pakistan
Citation: PLD 1988 SC 416
Court: Supreme Court of Pakistan
Year: 1988
Legal Issue: Whether the right to form and be a member of a political party is a fundamental right under Article 17 of the Constitution.
Facts: The petitioner challenged certain provisions restricting political party activities.
Decision: The Supreme Court held that the right to form and be a member of a political party is a fundamental right guaranteed under Article 17 of the Constitution. Restrictions on political parties must be reasonable and in accordance with law.
Relevant Law: Constitution of Pakistan, Article 17 (Freedom of Association).
Principle: Freedom of association includes the right to form political parties. Restrictions must be reasonable.
Source: Supreme Court of Pakistan official reports.

[SECTION: CASE_DARSHAN_MASIH_V_STATE]
Case: Darshan Masih v. The State
Citation: PLD 1990 SC 513
Court: Supreme Court of Pakistan
Year: 1990
Legal Issue: Bonded labour and fundamental rights under Articles 9, 11, and 14 of the Constitution.
Facts: Bonded labourers in brick kilns petitioned the Supreme Court for release from bonded servitude.
Decision: The Supreme Court declared all forms of bonded labour unconstitutional and violative of fundamental rights. The Court directed the government to take steps to eliminate bonded labour practices.
Relevant Law: Constitution of Pakistan, Articles 9, 11, 14; Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act, 1992.
Principle: Bonded labour is a violation of the constitutional right to life, liberty, and human dignity.
Source: Supreme Court of Pakistan official reports.
Source URL: https://www.supremecourt.gov.pk

[SECTION: CASE_SHEHLA_ZIA_V_WAPDA]
Case: Shehla Zia v. WAPDA
Citation: PLD 1994 SC 693
Court: Supreme Court of Pakistan
Year: 1994
Legal Issue: Right to life under Article 9 includes right to a clean and healthy environment.
Facts: Residents challenged the construction of a high-voltage grid station near residential areas, claiming health hazards.
Decision: The Supreme Court broadened the interpretation of 'right to life' under Article 9 to include the right to a clean and unpolluted environment. The Court ordered WAPDA to commission a study on health effects.
Relevant Law: Constitution of Pakistan, Article 9.
Principle: The right to life under Article 9 encompasses the right to a healthy and clean environment.
Source: Supreme Court of Pakistan official reports.

[SECTION: CASE_PAKISTAN_V_PUBLIC_AT_LARGE]
Case: Pakistan v. Public at Large (Suo Motu)
Citation: PLD 2013 SC 279
Court: Supreme Court of Pakistan
Year: 2013
Legal Issue: Enforced disappearances and the constitutional rights of detained persons.
Facts: The Supreme Court took suo motu notice of multiple cases of enforced disappearances across Pakistan.
Decision: The Court held that enforced disappearances violate Articles 9, 10, and 14 of the Constitution. The Court directed intelligence agencies to produce missing persons and established a Commission of Inquiry on Enforced Disappearances.
Relevant Law: Constitution of Pakistan, Articles 9, 10, 14, 199.
Principle: Every person detained by the State has the right to be produced before a court. Enforced disappearances are unconstitutional.
Source: Supreme Court of Pakistan official reports.
Source URL: https://www.supremecourt.gov.pk

[SECTION: CASE_KHAKI_V_RAWALPINDI]
Case: Dr. Muhammad Aslam Khaki v. SSP (Operations) Rawalpindi
Citation: Islamabad High Court, 2010
Court: Islamabad High Court
Year: 2010
Legal Issue: Transgender persons' rights and legal identity.
Facts: Petition filed for recognition of rights of transgender persons (khawaja sira) in Pakistan.
Decision: The Supreme Court recognized the fundamental rights of transgender persons including the right to identity, dignity, education, and inheritance. NADRA was directed to issue identity cards.
Relevant Law: Constitution of Pakistan, Articles 9, 14, 25.
Principle: Transgender persons are entitled to all fundamental rights guaranteed by the Constitution.
Source: Supreme Court of Pakistan official reports.

[SECTION: CASE_SALMAN_AKRAM_RAJA]
Case: Salman Akram Raja v. Government of Punjab
Citation: PLD 2013 SC 111
Court: Supreme Court of Pakistan
Year: 2013
Legal Issue: Right to fair trial and due process under Article 10A.
Facts: Challenge to the proceedings of special tribunals and military courts.
Decision: The Supreme Court affirmed that Article 10A guarantees the right to a fair trial to every person, and this right cannot be curtailed by special procedures unless they meet the minimum standards of fairness.
Relevant Law: Constitution of Pakistan, Article 10A.
Principle: Right to fair trial is an inviolable fundamental right that applies to all judicial and quasi-judicial proceedings.
Source: Supreme Court of Pakistan official reports.

[SECTION: CASE_IMRANA_TIWANA]
Case: Imrana Tiwana v. Province of Punjab
Citation: 2015 SCMR 1739
Court: Supreme Court of Pakistan
Year: 2015
Legal Issue: Right to access public parks and preservation of government property.
Facts: Government of Punjab sought to convert Jilani Park (formerly Racecourse Park) in Lahore for commercial purposes.
Decision: The Supreme Court held that public parks and green spaces are held in trust for citizens and cannot be commercialized or privatized. The right to a healthy environment under Article 9 includes access to parks.
Relevant Law: Constitution of Pakistan, Articles 9, 14; Punjab Parks and Green Areas Ordinance.
Principle: Public parks are held in public trust and cannot be converted for private commercial use.
Source: Supreme Court of Pakistan official reports.
Source URL: https://www.supremecourt.gov.pk

[SECTION: CASE_MUHAMMAD_NAWAZ_SHARIF]
Case: Muhammad Nawaz Sharif v. President of Pakistan
Citation: PLD 1993 SC 473
Court: Supreme Court of Pakistan
Year: 1993
Legal Issue: Dissolution of National Assembly under Article 58(2)(b) and judicial review.
Facts: The President dissolved the National Assembly. The deposed Prime Minister challenged the dissolution.
Decision: The Supreme Court restored the National Assembly, holding that the President had failed to demonstrate adequate grounds for dissolution. The Court exercised judicial review over executive actions.
Relevant Law: Constitution of Pakistan, Article 58(2)(b) (since repealed by 18th Amendment), Article 184(3).
Principle: Dissolution of the National Assembly is subject to judicial review. The executive must justify its actions before the courts.
Source: Supreme Court of Pakistan official reports.
Source URL: https://www.supremecourt.gov.pk
