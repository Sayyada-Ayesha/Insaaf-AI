[SECTION: dual_nationality_pakistan] Pakistan's Citizenship Act, 1951 governs Pakistani nationality. Pakistan permits DUAL NATIONALITY only with a defined list of countries announced by the Federal Government, and the list is subject to change by official notification. A Pakistani who acquires a nationality that is NOT on the permitted list may lose Pakistani citizenship by operation of law, and a person ceasing to be a Pakistani has different rights (for example regarding certain property, franchise or government posts). Because the permitted-countries list and the rules change by official notification, the current list must be confirmed from MOFA / the relevant federal notification (https://www.mofa.gov.pk) and NADRA (https://nadra.gov.pk) rather than assumed. A Pakistani passport may only be held/used by a person who is a Pakistani citizen; using another country's passport to enter Pakistan has its own rules. INSAaf does not fabricate the current list of countries. Status: CURRENT. Verified: 2026-09-04.

[SECTION: foreign_marriage_recognition] Whether a marriage validly contracted abroad is recognised in Pakistan (and vice versa) depends on BOTH jurisdictions: the law of the country where the marriage took place (which made it valid there) and Pakistani law on recognition, registration and (for Muslims) the requirements under the Muslim Family Laws Ordinance, 1961 and registration/NADRA recording. A foreign marriage certificate usually needs to be properly attested/legalised to be used in Pakistan, and registration of the marriage with Pakistani authorities may be required for full recognition for purposes like immigration, inheritance or spousal rights. Recognition is not automatic for every foreign marriage — it turns on the specific facts and applicable Pakistani rules. This is a cross-border matter requiring both the foreign documentation side and the Pakistan recognition side; verify current requirements with NADRA / the relevant mission. Status: CURRENT. Verified: 2026-09-04.

[SECTION: foreign_divorce_recognition] A divorce obtained abroad is not automatically effective in Pakistan; its recognition depends on Pakistani law and, for Muslims, procedural requirements (such as notice/registration relevant under the Muslim Family Laws framework) and the circumstances of the foreign decree. Conversely, a Pakistani divorce may need recognition abroad under that country's rules. Cross-border divorce frequently raises parallel questions of maintenance, dower, iddat and child custody, each of which may have a Pakistan-side and a foreign-side aspect. Because recognition of foreign decrees is legally technical and can affect remarriage and inheritance, the safe route is to confirm recognition through the proper Pakistani authority/mission and, where needed, obtain advice from a qualified lawyer. INSAaf gives the routing and cautions, not a definitive recognition opinion on facts it lacks. Status: CURRENT. Verified: 2026-09-04.

[SECTION: cross_border_child_custody] Child custody / guardianship spanning Pakistan and another country is a genuinely cross-border, jurisdiction-sensitive issue: which court has authority, which country's welfare standard applies, and whether a custody order from one country will be enforced in the other, are all determined by the relevant laws and any applicable international arrangements (for example, abduction/custody conventions as they apply between the specific states — not all countries are parties). International child removal/retention can carry serious legal consequences under the foreign country's law. Such matters should be handled by qualified lawyers in the relevant country or countries, with consular notification where a Pakistani minor is involved (directory via https://www.mofa.gov.pk). INSAaf explains the jurisdiction split and routes to authority; it does not opine on a specific custody outcome. Status: CURRENT. Verified: 2026-09-04.

[SECTION: foreign_property_matters] Owning, buying, selling or transferring property in a FOREIGN country is governed by that country's property, tax and (often) foreign-ownership rules — not Pakistani law. A Pakistani resident dealing with foreign property may need documents (powers of attorney, contracts) executed and attested abroad, and must comply with the foreign country's registration and tax requirements. Repatriating sale proceeds and any Pakistani-side reporting/tax treatment is a separate question governed by Pakistan's rules (State Bank / FBR — https://www.sbp.org.pk, https://fbr.gov.pk) and any applicable double-taxation arrangement. Both jurisdictions can apply to a single transaction. INSAaf does not state a specific country's foreign-ownership rules; it identifies the two layers and points to each side's official authority. Status: CURRENT. Verified: 2026-09-04.

[SECTION: cross_border_inheritance] Inheritance where the deceased or heirs are Pakistani but there are assets, or a death, abroad is a cross-border matter engaging (A) the foreign country's succession/probate rules for assets located there and (B) Pakistani succession law (including Muslim personal law of inheritance, and the applicable family/personal law for non-Muslims) for assets in Pakistan, plus recognition of foreign grant-of-probate/letters of administration. Which law applies to which asset often depends on where the asset is situated (immovable property typically follows the law of the country where it is located) and the applicable personal law. Because foreign grants must sometimes be recognised in Pakistan (and vice versa) under the relevant rules, professional assistance and coordination with the Pakistani mission/authorities is usually needed. INSAaf maps the jurisdiction split; it does not compute shares without the verified facts and applicable law. Status: CURRENT. Verified: 2026-09-04.

[SECTION: foreign_court_document] A document received FROM a FOREIGN court or authority — such as a U.S. court notice/summons, a foreign claim, an immigration/visa decision letter, a foreign police notice, or a deportation/removal notice — is governed by the ISSUING foreign country's law and procedure, NOT by Pakistani law. The correct response is determined by: (1) what the document actually commands (appear, respond, pay, leave), (2) the deadline it states, (3) the foreign authority/court named, and (4) that country's own procedure — usually requiring a qualified LOCAL lawyer in that country. Pakistani authorities do not adjudicate foreign court proceedings; a Pakistani mission may give consular support (contact list, welfare) via https://www.mofa.gov.pk but cannot act on the foreign case. Ignoring a foreign court deadline can carry real consequences under the foreign legal system. Treat the foreign document on its own terms and never apply Pakistani court procedure to it. Status: CURRENT. Verified: 2026-09-04.

[SECTION: foreign_judgment_enforcement] Enforcing a judgment obtained in one country in another (for example enforcing a foreign money decree in Pakistan, or a Pakistani decree abroad) is not automatic. Pakistan's Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 deals with recognition/enforcement of foreign judgments, distinguishing "reciprocating" territories from others, and enforcement generally requires a court process in the country where enforcement is sought, subject to that country's limits (e.g., public policy, jurisdiction of the original court). The foreign side applies its own enforcement rules. Because this is technical and jurisdiction-pair-specific, professional representation in the enforcing country is normally required. INSAaf identifies the correct framework and the need for local counsel; it does not promise enforcement. Status: CURRENT. Verified: 2026-09-04.

[SECTION: cross_border_tax_routing] Tax duties of a Pakistani living or earning abroad can involve BOTH countries: tax residence, source-country withholding, and Pakistan's own tax treatment of overseas income and remittances, potentially modified by any Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (DTAA/tax treaty) between Pakistan and the other state. Pakistan's tax authority is the Federal Board of Revenue (https://fbr.gov.pk). Whether a person is a Pakistani tax resident, what is taxable, and what treaty relief exists are precise, changeable legal/financial determinations. INSAaf does not give tax advice or invent treaty terms; it flags the cross-border tax dimension and routes to FBR and the host country's official tax authority (and recommends a qualified tax adviser). Status: CURRENT. Verified: 2026-09-04.

[SECTION: cross_border_remedies_routing] A cross-border legal problem should be triaged by jurisdiction before any advice: identify (1) where the person is, (2) where the counterparty/asset/event is, (3) which country's law governs the issue, then use the RIGHT forum for each layer — the foreign country's courts/authorities for foreign-law matters, Pakistani authorities (courts, NADRA, MOFA, BE&OE/FBR) for Pakistan-law matters, and consular channels for citizen welfare. The immediate safety step comes first in emergencies (arrest, trafficking, danger). This routing principle is central: the system must NOT default to Pakistani law merely because the user is Pakistani. Status: CURRENT. Verified: 2026-09-04.
