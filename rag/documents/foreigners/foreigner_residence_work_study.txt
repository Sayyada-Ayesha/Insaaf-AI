# Foreigners in Pakistan — Residence, Registration, Work, Study, Family and Emergencies
# Depth expansion over foreigner_entry_stay_registration.txt and pakistan_visa_categories.txt.
# Every record distinguishes PAKISTAN LAW (the Foreigners Act 1946, the Pakistan Online Visa
# System, citizenship law) from the FOREIGNER'S HOME-COUNTRY LAW (consular protection, own
# embassy services) — the two are never conflated below.

[SECTION: FOREIGN_RESIDENCE_FRAMEWORK]
PAKISTAN LAW: Foreigners Act, 1946 (as applicable) and the Ministry of Interior's visa and
residence framework. COVERAGE: PARTIAL.
KEY FRAMEWORK (summary):
- Entry: foreigners enter on a valid visa (Pakistan Online Visa System) or visa-free
  arrangements; the visa states duration of stay and number of entries.
- Registration: foreigners staying beyond the visa-free period may be required to register
  with the police under the Foreigners Act, 1946 framework (Foreigners Registration
  requirement) — check current MoI practice.
- Residence permit: long-stay foreigners (work/study/business) obtain a Residence Permit
  from the Ministry of Interior; the permit evidences lawful residence and is renewable.
- Pakistan Origin Card (POC): available to foreign nationals of Pakistani origin (including
  former citizens) for visa-free entry and indefinite stay, subject to the POC Rules.
SOURCE: Foreigners Act, 1946; MoI visa/residence practice. VERIFICATION: metadata-verified
(exact current registration and permit procedures change; check MoI's official pages).

[SECTION: FOREIGN_VISA_EXTENSION]
PAKISTAN LAW: visa extension and re-entry. COVERAGE: PARTIAL.
KEY FRAMEWORK (summary):
- Extensions: visa extensions are applied to the Ministry of Interior (visa extension
  process) before expiry; overstay without extension is an immigration offence (fine,
  detention and/or deportation exposure).
- Exit/re-entry: a single-entry visa does not permit re-entry; multi-entry visas state the
  re-entry conditions; departure before expiry and re-entry on a fresh visa is the standard
  route; exit permits may be required in specific cases (e.g., where an exit permit is
  endorsed).
- Documentation to carry: passport with valid visa/stamp, residence permit where issued,
  and proof of registration where required.
SOURCE: MoI visa rules. VERIFICATION: metadata-verified.

[SECTION: FOREIGN_WORK_STUDY_BUSINESS]
PAKISTAN LAW: work, study and business categories under the Online Visa System. COVERAGE: PARTIAL.
KEY FRAMEWORK (summary):
- Work: a work visa requires a sponsoring Pakistani employer/invitation letter and sectoral
  clearance where required (e.g., Board of Investment recommendation for employment);
  the visa is tied to the employer and period of employment.
- Business: business visas for investors/entrepreneurs with the prescribed invitation and
  documentation; long-stay business activity may require the residence permit route.
- Study: study visas for students enrolled at HEC-recognized institutions, with admission
  letter and, where required, No-Objection Certificate; age/eligibility per the current
  rules.
- Each category has its own document list in the Online Visa System — always use the
  official checklist for the category.
SOURCE: Pakistan Online Visa System categories. VERIFICATION: metadata-verified (category
checklists change; the visa portal is the authoritative source).

[SECTION: FOREIGN_POLICE_IMMIGRATION]
PAKISTAN LAW: interaction with police and immigration authorities. COVERAGE: PARTIAL.
KEY FRAMEWORK (summary):
- Routine checks: foreigners may be asked to show passport, visa and registration documents
  by police/immigration officials; carrying the passport or attested copies with the visa
  page is advisable.
- Offences: overstay, breach of visa conditions (unauthorized work on a study visa etc.) and
  entry without valid documents are immigration offences; remedies include fine,
  imprisonment for serious cases, and deportation.
- Arrest/detention: a detained foreigner has the right to legal representation under
  Pakistani law and to consular notification — the foreigner's embassy/consulate is
  contacted (home-country consular channel, not a Pakistani-law remedy).
- Registration complaints: complaints about police/immigration conduct go to the
  department, the ombudsman, or the courts (see civic-services complaint workflow).
SOURCE: Foreigners Act, 1946; CrPC rights framework. VERIFICATION: metadata-verified.

[SECTION: FOREIGN_FAMILY_MATTERS]
PAKISTAN LAW vs HOME-COUNTRY LAW: family matters. COVERAGE: PARTIAL.
KEY FRAMEWORK (summary):
- Marriage: a marriage in Pakistan involving a foreign national is registered under
  Pakistani law (nikahnama) where performed in Pakistan; marriage to a Pakistani citizen
  does NOT by itself confer Pakistani citizenship.
- Citizenship through marriage (Citizenship Act, 1951 framework): a foreign woman married
  to a Pakistani citizen may acquire citizenship on the prescribed conditions; a foreign
  man married to a Pakistani woman does not gain citizenship through the marriage alone
  (gender-specific historical rule — verify the current act).
- Divorce/child custody: governed by Pakistani family law where the matter is within
  Pakistani jurisdiction; a foreign court order may need recognition/execution in Pakistan,
  and vice versa — the applicable law depends on jurisdiction (see cross-border family
  records).
- Birth of a child: children born in Pakistan to foreign parents do not automatically
  become Pakistani citizens (citizenship is by descent/documented grounds under the
  Citizenship Act, 1951).
SOURCE: Citizenship Act, 1951; family-law framework. VERIFICATION: metadata-verified
(citizenship conditions should be checked against the current act).

[SECTION: FOREIGN_EMERGENCY_CONS]
DISTINCTION: emergency and consular support. COVERAGE: PARTIAL.
- PAKISTAN SIDE: emergency services (Rescue 1122, hospitals, police 15) serve everyone in
  Pakistan regardless of nationality; the foreigner pays for medical treatment like any
  private patient unless covered.
- HOME-COUNTRY SIDE: consular protection (lost passport, emergency travel document, legal
  referrals, welfare checks) is provided by the foreigner's own embassy/consulate — this is
  the home country's service, NOT a Pakistani government function. Pakistan's Ministry of
  Foreign Affairs facilitates access to foreign missions but does not provide consular
  services to foreign nationals.
- Lost documents: report the loss to the local police (FIR/duly reported) and to the home
  embassy; the embassy issues replacement/emergency travel documents, then the Ministry of
  Interior/immigration is approached for exit formalities where required.
SOURCE: MOFA facilitation practice; consular practice of foreign missions.
VERIFICATION: metadata-verified.

[SECTION: FOREIGN_HOME_COUNTRY_LAW_NOTE]
RULE FOR ANSWERS: whenever a question concerns what a foreigner's OWN country can or must do
(consular assistance, home-country documents, home-country marriage/divorce rules), answer
from the home country's framework and say it is home-country law — do not answer it with
Pakistani law. Pakistani law governs entry, stay, registration, offences and remedies within
Pakistan.
