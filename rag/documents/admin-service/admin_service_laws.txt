# Administrative Law and Public Service — Pakistan
# Coverage: civil service terms (Civil Servants Act 1973), Service Tribunals, efficiency and
# discipline proceedings, judicial review of administrative action, and citizen remedies.
# Sources: Civil Servants Act, 1973; Service Tribunals Act, 1973; Government Servants
# (Efficiency and Discipline) Rules, 1973; Constitution of Pakistan, Article 199.

[SECTION: ADMIN_READING_NOTE]
Administrative law governs how public authorities and civil servants must act, and what a
citizen can do when an authority acts unlawfully. The two strands are: (1) service law (terms
of employment and discipline of civil servants) and (2) judicial review (courts checking
administrative decisions for legality under the Constitution).

[SECTION: ADMIN_CIVIL_SERVANTS_ACT]
LAW: Civil Servants Act, 1973. COVERAGE: PARTIAL.
KEY PROVISIONS (summary of the operative framework):
- Applies to civil servants in federal service (provincial civil servants are governed by
  provincial civil servants acts modelled on the same scheme).
- Terms and conditions: appointment, probation, seniority, promotion, transfer and posting are
  regulated under the Act and rules.
- Termination of service: termination, removal from service and compulsory retirement are
  distinct penalties with different legal consequences; removal from service imposed after
  departmental proceedings is a major penalty.
- Grounds of dismissal: misconduct and inefficiency are dealt with under the Efficiency and
  Discipline Rules.
SOURCE: Civil Servants Act, 1973. VERIFICATION: verified as to framework.

[SECTION: ADMIN_ED_RULES]
LAW: Government Servants (Efficiency and Discipline) Rules, 1973 (and provincial equivalents).
COVERAGE: PARTIAL.
KEY PROVISIONS (summary):
- Show-cause notice: before imposing a major penalty (reduction to lower post, removal,
  dismissal), the authority must issue a show-cause notice stating the charges and the basis
  of the charge and give a reasonable opportunity to reply.
- Inquiry: where the reply is unsatisfactory, a factual inquiry may be conducted; the accused
  officer can defend, produce evidence and cross-examine (subject to the rules).
- Penalties: minor (censure, withholding of promotion) and major (reduction, removal, dismissal).
- Appeal/review: orders are appealable to the appellate authority under the rules.
SOURCE: E&D Rules, 1973. VERIFICATION: verified as to the show-cause/inquiry/appeal structure.

[SECTION: ADMIN_SERVICE_TRIBUNALS]
LAW: Service Tribunals Act, 1973 (Federal Service Tribunal; provincial service tribunals).
COVERAGE: PARTIAL.
KEY PROVISIONS (summary):
- The Federal Service Tribunal hears appeals of federal civil servants against departmental
  orders (dismissal, removal, reduction etc.); provincial tribunals for provincial servants.
- Aggrieved civil servants must exhaust the departmental appeal before the Tribunal where
  required, then appeal to the Tribunal within the limitation period.
- Tribunal decisions are final subject to the Supreme Court's jurisdiction; the High Court's
  jurisdiction over service matters is barred by the Act, leaving the Supreme Court as the
  superior forum.
SOURCE: Service Tribunals Act, 1973. VERIFICATION: verified as to framework.

[SECTION: ADMIN_JUDICIAL_REVIEW]
LAW: Constitution of Pakistan, Article 199 (High Courts) and Article 184(3) (Supreme Court
original jurisdiction). COVERAGE: PARTIAL.
KEY PROVISIONS (close paraphrase):
- The High Court may, on application of an aggrieved person, direct a person performing
  functions in connection with the affairs of the Federation, a Province or a local authority
  to refrain from doing an act not permitted by law, or to do an act required by law, or declare
  an act done without lawful authority void — the constitutional writ jurisdiction
  (mandamus, prohibition, certiorari, quo warranto and habeas corpus).
- Habeas corpus: for release of a person illegally detained.
- Grounds of review include: lack of jurisdiction, error of law apparent on the record, breach
  of natural justice (audi alteram partem — right to be heard; rule against bias), and mala
  fides (bad faith).
SOURCE: Constitution of Pakistan, Article 199. VERIFICATION: verified (writ framework is
well-established; see the rights-corpus for the fundamental-rights provisions).

[SECTION: ADMIN_NATURAL_JUSTICE]
PRINCIPLES (summary of the settled position in Pakistani administrative law):
- Audi alteram partem: no person may be condemned unheard; before an order adverse to a person
  (removal from service, cancellation of licence, blacklisting), that person must be given
  notice and an opportunity to be heard.
- Rule against bias: a decision-maker with a personal interest in the matter must not decide it
  (nemo judex in causa sua).
- Reasoned orders: administrative orders affecting rights should state reasons where required.
- These principles are judicially recognized; breach makes the order liable to be set aside in
  writ jurisdiction.
SOURCE: settled case law. VERIFICATION: verified as settled principles.

[SECTION: ADMIN_CITIZEN_REMEDIES]
CITIZEN REMEDY ROUTES (summary):
- Departmental complaint: written complaint to the head of the department/authority first.
- Ombudsman: Federal Ombudsman (Wafaqi Mohtasib) for maladministration in federal agencies;
  provincial ombudsmen for provincial agencies (see civic-services records).
- Writ petition: where the administrative action is unlawful, a constitutional petition to the
  High Court (Article 199) or a civil suit where a private right is affected.
- Regulatory complaints: sector regulators (DRAP, healthcare commissions, food authorities,
  PTA, NEPRA, OGRA etc.) for sector-specific grievances.
SOURCE: Constitution and ombudsman legislation. VERIFICATION: verified as to routes.
