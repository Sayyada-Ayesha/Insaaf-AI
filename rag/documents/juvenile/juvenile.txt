[SECTION: 1] Juvenile Justice System Ordinance, 2000 (Ordinance No. XXII of 2000) — Provides for the protection, treatment and rehabilitation of juvenile offenders. Source: Pakistan Code (pakistancode.gov.pk). Status: CURRENT (amended by JJSO Amendment Act 2018).

[SECTION: 2] Definitions — 'Juvenile' means a person who has not attained the age of 18 years. 'Juvenile offender' means a juvenile who has been charged with or found guilty of an offence. Male juveniles (under 18) and female juveniles (under 18) are covered.

[SECTION: 3] Separation of juveniles — Juvenile offenders must be kept separate from adult offenders in police stations, jails and other custodial facilities. No juvenile shall be kept in a police lockup for more than 24 hours.

[SECTION: 4] Arrest procedure — The officer arresting a juvenile must immediately inform the parent or guardian. If no parent/guardian available, the officer must inform the Juvenile Court or probation officer.

[SECTION: 5] Juvenile Court — Special Juvenile Courts may be established. Where no separate Juvenile Court exists, the District and Sessions Judge or a Magistrate designated by the High Court conducts juvenile proceedings. Proceedings are conducted in camera.

[SECTION: 6] Bail for juveniles — Juveniles charged with bailable or non-bailable offences may be released on bail, unless there are reasonable grounds that release would expose the juvenile to moral or physical danger or defeat the ends of justice.

[SECTION: 7] Disposal of cases — Cases involving juveniles must be disposed of as expeditiously as possible. If not disposed of within 4 months, the court must record reasons for delay.

[SECTION: 8] Probation and supervision — The court may release a juvenile on probation under the supervision of a probation officer. Conditions may include attendance at school, vocational training, or residence at a specified place.

[SECTION: 9] Sentencing of juveniles — No juvenile shall be sentenced to death, life imprisonment, or imprisonment for more than 7 years. The preferred disposition is: (a) release on advice/admonition; (b) release on probation; (c) community service; (d) custody in a juvenile rehabilitation center; (e) fine or compensation.

[SECTION: 10] Rehabilitation centers — The government must establish and maintain juvenile rehabilitation centers. Centers provide education, vocational training, medical care and counseling. Juveniles are segregated by age and gender.

[SECTION: 11] Probation officers — Probation officers investigate family and social background of juvenile offenders and supervise juveniles released on probation. Reports are submitted to the court.

[SECTION: 12] Prohibition of handcuffing — No juvenile shall be handcuffed or chained. Restraint may only be used in extreme circumstances with court permission.

[SECTION: 13] Education and training — Juvenile rehabilitation centers must provide: (a) basic education up to at least middle level; (b) vocational training in trades; (c) religious and moral instruction.

[SECTION: 14] Aftercare — Aftercare programs assist juveniles released from rehabilitation centers to reintegrate into society. Aftercare includes support for employment, continued education, and family reunification.

[SECTION: 15] Child protection — The Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Child Protection and Welfare Act 2010, Punjab Child Protection and Welfare Bureau, and Sindh Child Protection Authority Act provide additional child protection mechanisms.

[SECTION: 16] Child abuse offences — PPC Sections: (a) Section 292: child pornography; (b) Section 293: sale of obscene objects to young persons; (c) Section 316-A: causing death of unborn child; (d) Section 328-B: child abandonment; (e) Section 369-A: kidnapping or abducting a child under 14.

[SECTION: 17] Corporal punishment — Corporal punishment in schools and institutions is prohibited by various provincial laws. The right to education Article 25-A and child protection laws prohibit physical punishment.

[SECTION: 18] Registration of child abuse cases — FIR must be registered for offences against children. Special investigation units (Child Protection Units) exist in some provinces. Testimony through video-link or screens may be permitted.
