POLICY ON DUAL, DOUBLE AND JOINT DEGREE PROGRAM ME S HEC Policy on Dual, Double and Joint Degree Programmes Foreword The Higher Education Commission, in pursuance of its mandate to regulate and facilitate higher education in Pakistan, continuously strives to strengthen academic quality, relevance and global compatibility of the country’s higher education system. In recen t years, collaborative academic arrangements such as Dual, Double and Joint Degree Programmes have gained increasing importance internationally as mechanisms for promoting academic cooperation, student mobility and shared learning opportunities.
Recognizin g these emerging trends, HEC has formulated the “Policy on Dual, Double and Joint Degree Programmes” to provide a clear and structured framework for collaboration between Pakistani Higher Education Institutions and recognized local and foreign partner inst itutions. The policy outlines the key principles, regulatory requirements and operational mechanisms necessary for the offering of collaborative degree program me s while ensuring quality assurance, institutional accountability and transparency.
The policy h as been developed through extensive consultations with universities and relevant stakeholders, while also taking into consideration international best practices and evolving academic needs. It aims to facilitate academic collaboration, strengthen research linkages, improve international engagement and enhance the employability and global recognition of graduates.
It is hoped that this framework will enable Higher Education Institutions to establish innovative, sustainable and mutually beneficial partnership s that contribute positively towards the development and internationalization of higher education in Pakistan.
Meritorious Prof. Dr. Zia U l Haq, S.I.
Executive Director, HEC Engr. Prof. Dr. Niaz Ahmad Akhtar, S.I.
Chairman, HEC HEC Policy on Dual, Double and Joint Degree Programmes Preamble Collaborative degree programmes have emerged globally as an important avenue of higher education internationalization, enabling institutions to strengthen academic cooperation through the sharing of expertise, resources, research, and educational opportuni ties.
Recognizing the growing significance of such collaborations at the tertiary education level, the Higher Education Commission (HEC) of Pakistan seeks to facilitate structured academic partnerships between Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) in Pakist an and their national and international counterparts, while ensuring academic quality, institutional accountability, and regulatory compliance.
Accordingly, i n exercise of the powers conferred under Clauses 10(1)(k) and 10(1)(t) of the Higher Education Com mission Ordinance, 2002 (LIII of 2002), HEC has devised the “Policy on Dual, Double, and Joint Degree Programmes.” This Policy shall apply to public and private HEIs intending to collaborate with local and/or foreign HEIs for the award of dual, double, and joint degree qualifications at the undergraduate and graduate levels.
The Policy aims to provide a regulatory and operational framework for academic collaborations established through formal instruments or agreements for the purposes of:
i. Dual Degree Program me (s)
ii. Double Degree Program me (s)
iii. Joint Degree Program me (s)
Definitions “Collaborative Agreement” means an arrangement under which tuition, training, or academic facilitation is provided by two or more partnering institutions through a jointly designed Scheme of Studies duly approved by the statutory bodies of each collaborating institution. The asses sment process shall be carried out by the partnering institutions in line with applicable national standards, leading to the award of one or more degree(s).
“Commission” means the Higher Education Commission (HEC) established under Ordinance LIII of 2002.
“Consecutive Study” means a mode of study in which a student pursues an academic programme on a continuous, uninterrupted basis, progressing from one term/semester or academic level to the next without any approved break, until completion of the qualificat ion.
“Diploma Supplement” means a document attached to the degree certificate or transcript that describes the nature, level, context, content, and status of the studies that were pursued and successfully completed, including the collaborative nature of th e programme, the partnering institutions involved, and the credit distribution across institutions.
“Double Degree” means two degrees in the same discipline awarded by partnering institutions.
“Dual Degree” means two degrees in distinct or related fields a warded either by a single institution or by partnering institutions.
HEC Policy on Dual, Double and Joint Degree Programmes “Foreign Higher Education Institution” means an HEI established outside Pakistan that is listed in the World Higher Education Database (WHED) maintained by the United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO) , or is recognized in its country of origin , and offers recognized undergraduate and/or graduate academic and research degree program me s.

3. Institutional Oversight and Corrective Measures
Programmes approved under this Policy shall be subject to periodic quality assurance monitoring and audits by HEC to ensure continued compliance with applicable academic, regulatory, and quality assurance standards. During the initial three years of programme implementation, HEC’s relevant divisions may conduct annual quality assurance reviews focusing on, inter alia, student outcomes, curriculum delivery, institutional support mechanisms, and adherence to approved programme requirements. Suc h reviews and audits shall be conducted in accordance with predefined quality assurance parameters, benchmarks, and review mechanisms prescribed by HEC, including those set out under the Monitoring and Evaluation Framework (Annexure - III).
HEC Policy on Dual, Double and Joint Degree Programmes Where concerns re lating to adherence to the prescribed requirements are identified, HEC may adopt appropriate facilitative and regulatory measures, including enhanced monitoring or, where necessary, review of collaborative arrangements in accordance with applicable regulat ions. Such matters shall, as far as possible, be addressed through a graduated approach, including: (a) issuance of advisory observations along with a corrective action plan for minor deficiencies; (b) placement under enhanced monitoring with periodic repo rting requirements for matters requiring continued improvement; (c) temporary restriction on new enrolments pending implementation of remedial measures in cases of significant concern; and
(d) discontinuation of the collaborative arrangement in cases invol ving persistent or serious
irregularities.
Institutions shall be afforded reasonable notice and an opportunity to present their position before any adverse measure is finalized. Any action beyond issuance of advisory observations shall be considered by a c ommittee comprising at least three members designated by the Executive Director, HEC.
4 . Entry and Exit Requirements
Requirements for entering and exiting Dual/Double/Joint Degree Program me s shall be mutually established and finalized by the partnering institutions . In addition, the following safeguards shall also be established for the students :
i. Teach - Out Plan: Before NOC issuance, partnering institutions shall submit a Teach -
Out Plan to HEC specifying the arrangements for enrolled students i n the event that either institution withdraws from the partnership or the programme is discontinued. The plan shall guarantee that all enrolled students can complete their degree programme or, where completion is not feasible, receive a full refund of fees paid and transfer of credits to an equivalent programme.
ii. Minimum Notice Period: Either of the partnering institution (s) intending to withdraw
from the collaborative arrangement shall provide at least twelve months’ written notice to the other institution ( s) and to HEC. No new student enrolments shall be undertaken following issuance of such notice.
iii. Individual Exit Pathway: Students who are unable to complete the collaborative
programme requirements shall be provided with a defined exit pathway, which may i nclude the award of a single degree from the home institution, transfer of credits to another programme, or a certificate of completed coursework .
5 . Financial Arrangements
Clear financial arrangements, fee structures, and quality assurance mechanisms shall be defined by partnering institutions .
In addition, the following financial transparency requirements shall apply :
i. The complete fee structure for the entire duration of t he programme, including , inter
alia, tuition fees , mobility - related costs, examination fees , shall be disclosed to prospective students before enrolment and such structure shall also stand incorporated into the Mo C .

ii. Any increase in fee shall be capped at a rate agreed in the Mo C and disclosed to
students at the time of admission. Unilateral fee increases by either institution shall not be permissible during the enrolled cohort’s programme duration.
iii. The Mo C shall specify refund policies in the event of progr amme discontinuation or
student withdrawal, consistent with the Teach - Out Plan provisions under Section 4 .
iv. Partnering institutions shall submit , as part of their NOC application , a financial
sustainability assessment, demonstrating that the programme is financially viable for its intended duration.
6 . Curriculum and Program me Standards
Curriculum standards, program me objectives, and grading criteria shall be mutually established by the partnering institutions , ensuring compliance with the aca demic standards of the respective countries. Institutions shall also demonstrate how language competency is assured and supported throughout the programme.
Partnering institutions shall also comply with the procedural and academic coordination requirements set out in Annexure - II.

3. Assessment Parity
Partnering institutions shall ensure equivalence and consistency in assessment practices through harmonized grading scales, aligned assessment rubrics, and joint moderation mechanisms (e.g., 20% of exams co - evaluated by both institutions). Where appropriate, a portion of assessments may be jointly reviewed or co - evaluated by the partnering institutions to maintain academic integrity, fairness, and comparability of standards. Provisions shall also be included for external academic review in cases where significant discrepancies arise, with such arrangements being subject to review and approval by HEC, where applicable.
For this purpose, partnering institutions shall develop and implement appropriate mechanisms and guidelines relating to rubric harmonization and joint moderation of assessments, including the following:
Rubric Harmonization: Institutions shall develop and implement standardized assessment rubrics to promote transparency, consistency, and comparability in evaluation practices, including alignment of grading and CGPA frameworks where applicable.
Joint Moderation of Assessments: Partnering institutions shall undertake joint moderation of assessments to maintain assessment quality, fairness, and consistency across collaborative programmes.
HEC Policy on Dual, Double and Joint Degree Programmes ANNEXURE - III Monitoring a nd Evaluation Framework Periodic Review and Performanc e Indicators Programmes shall be subject to periodic review by HEC to assess academic quality, institutional effectiveness, and outcomes against agreed performance indicators.
