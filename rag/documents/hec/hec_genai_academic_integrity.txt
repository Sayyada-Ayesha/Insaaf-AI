1. Introduction
Generative AI (GenAI) refers to artificial intelligence (AI) systems capable of producing written, visual, audio, or code content in response to user prompts. These systems are designed to simulate human - like understanding and g enerate contextually appropriate responses. GenAI tools include but are not limited to Chat GPT - 5 , Claude, Gemini, DeepSeek, and similar large language models.
The emergence of such tools represents a significant technological shift with implications for te aching, learning, research, and administration in higher education.
Since 2023, generative AI technology has advanced dramatically with increased capabilities, accessibility, and real - world applications. This framework adopts a technology - agnostic approac h, focusing on principles rather than specific tools, ensuring relevance as AI capabilities continue to evolve. The rapid pace of AI development requires Higher Education Institutes ( HEIs ) to maintain flexibility and regularly review policies to reflect cu rrent technological reality.
This framework takes a balanced approach that protects academic integrity while enabling productive use of AI as a professional tool. Rather than broad prohibitions, the framework distinguishes misuse from responsible use, recognizes that AI literacy is essential for graduates, and empowers institutions to develop context - appropriate policies reflecting their educational mission.
2 . The HEC’s Mandate
In accordance with Section 10(a) in the Higher Education Commission (HEC) Ordinance of 2002 , the HEC is entrusted with the responsibility to promote, coordinate, and supervise university education and research in Pakistan, ensuring that educational standards are maintained and that institutions adhere to best practices in their academic operations. It further encourages the HEIs to make policies and priorities for guiding principles that support socio - economic development of the country.
The HEC recognizes that the adoption and use of generative AI tools in HEIs requires clear governance, transparent policies, and systematic monitoring. This framework provides guidance to ensure that AI use supports learning outcomes, maintains academic integrity, and prepares students for careers in an AI - integrated professional landscape. The framework will be reviewed every six months to assess emerging issues and incorporate updated guidance based on institutional experience and technological developments.
The HEC will issue quarterly guidance bulletins on emerging AI use cases, best practice s from member institutions, and implementation updates to support HEIs in navigating this rapidly evolving landscape.

3. Objectives
The framework addresses a variety of academic community members , including students, faculty, researchers, mentors , and academic staff , to demonstrate proper GenAI tool usage. The document presents possible destructive effects of GenAI technology on today's academic methods while establishing essential standards for ethical use throughout universities and their Degree Awarding Institutes ( DAIs ) and their respective colleges and research establishments.
The objectives of this framework are to:
i. Maintain academic integrity and ensure that assessment accurately reflects student
learning and understanding;
ii. Provide clear guid ance to students, faculty, and administrators on ethical and
appropriate use of generative AI tools;
iii. Ensure transparency in the use of AI tools to facilitate accountability and informed
decision - making;
iv. Protect student data privacy and ensure compliance wi th applicable laws and
regulations;
v. Promote fairness and prevent bias in the application of AI tools;
vi. Support institutional capacity building and professional development in AI literacy;
vii. Enable productive and responsible use of generative AI as a professi onal and
educational tool, recognizing that AI literacy is essential for graduate competency;
viii. Build institutional and student AI literacy, ensuring understanding of capabilities,
limitations, and ethical implications of generative AI; and
ix. Prepare graduates for careers in AI - integrated professions where generative AI use is a
standard practice, ensuring they develop critical judgment about appropriate and ethical use.
4 . Principles
The Framework operates under these main principles as its foundation :
 Every year , educational institutions alongside their teaching staff from Universities and DAIs must conduct skills development activities to build knowledge on Gen erative AI tool creation and methods to stop misuse.
 Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) need to d evelop specific formal permission systems for students and teaching staff when conducting Generative AI T ool implementations in their academic processes.
 Promise of Generative AI Tools lead to a dependency problem , which causes students and researchers to avoid critical thinking while delaying primary research , and stops them from developing their writing and creative talent.
 The training process of Large Language Models (LLMs) in Generative AI Tools on extensive datasets sometimes spread biased stereotype s and destructive narrative , which produce unethical content during academic use.
 All parties at Universities and DAI s along with faculty members students and staff should ad a pt research ethics to prevent plagiarism issues from Generative AI Tools in their scholarly work and research output .
i. Academic Integrity: Assessment and evaluation mechanisms must accurately measure
student learning and competency. AI - assisted work must be transparent, appropriately attributed, and aligned with learning objectives.
ii. Transparency: All stakeholders must understand when, how, and why AI tools are used
in learning, teaching, and research contexts.
iii. Informed Consent: Individuals should understand how their data is used in AI systems
and provide explicit consent where requir ed.
iv. Fairness and Equity: AI tools must not discriminate based on protected characteristics.
Institutions must monitor for and mitigate algorithmic bias.
v. Data Privacy: Collection, storage, and processing of personal data must comply with
applicable privacy laws and regulations.
vi. Accessibility: AI policies should not disadvantage students with disabilities. Institutions
must ensure equitable access to learning and assessment.
vii. AI as a Professional Tool: Transparent and responsible use of generative AI demonstrates
professional competency in contemporary workplaces. Using AI as a shortcut to avoid learning or deliberate misrepresentation is misuse; using AI as a documented tool for productivity and learning is responsible practice.
viii. Context - Dependent Gove rnance: Appropriate AI use varies by context.
Foundational learning contexts (first - year courses, core competencies) require stricter controls than research contexts. Assessment rules differ from collaboration tools. Policies must reflect this differentiat ion rather than applying blanket restrictions.
ix. Evidence - Based Enforcement: Detection of AI use alone is insufficient to establish
misconduct. Adverse actions require corroborating evidence of intent to misrepresent or circumvent learning objectives. Disclosure of AI use should not be an adverse factor in evaluation.
5 . Common Misuses and Responsible Use
This section distinguishes misuse of generative AI from responsible use. Misuse occurs when AI is used to avoid learning in foundational contexts, to deliberately misrepresent authorship, or to violate explicit course policies. Responsible use — with proper disclosure and learning intent — differs fundamentally and should be encouraged. 6 . Applicability
This Framework applies to all students together with university staff and faculty members , researchers , and personnel at Degree Awarding Institutes throughout Pakistan without sectoral distinctions. This guideline applies uniformly to both undergraduate studies and graduate courses.

7 . Institutional Responsibility
A . Transparency and Disclosure Transparency in the context of Generative AI Tools refers to the open communication and disclosure of the use of AI technologies within educational institutes. HEIs must establish clear institutional policies on the use of generative AI tools that are communicated to all students, faculty, and staff. These policies must specify what uses are permitted, prohibited, or require disclosure in different contexts (teaching, learning, assessment, research, administration).
Institutions must ensure that policie s are easily accessible and regularly updated.
B . Informed Consent Generative AI Tools imply both within information on the possible consequences of the use of Generative AI Tools and a voluntary choice on the application of the same. If Generative AI Tools are to be implemented in educational processes, then educational institutions must set up the processes to obtain informed consent from students and faculty. It includes defining the purposes, potential impacts , and the levels of risk associated with AI applications in the context of higher education. Specific details c an be obtained from Appendix 2.
C . Fairness and Bias Mitigation Institutions must ensure that AI tools are evaluated for potential bias before deployment.
Institutions should establish processes for monitoring and addressing discriminatory outcomes.
AI - based assessment tools should be audited for fairness, particularly regarding protected characteristics. Such a udits should be conducted regularly to identify and fix biases .
D . Data Privacy Data privacy is the protection of personal information of the students and faculty from unauthorized access, use , and disclosure. It should also be borne in mind that educational institutes must, as per stipulated regulations, ensure that the applicable data protection regulations are followed and that students a nd faculty data are protected by robust security measures. All the data used in Generative AI Tools/applications should be anonymized and always treated as confidential .
Data should not be transmitted to third parties without explicit consent. When using cloud - based AI services, institutions should verify data protection standards and ensure compliance with applicable laws, including the Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act (PECA), 2016 and the Draft Personal Data Protection Bill (PDPB), 2023 if/when approv ed .
E . Accessibility Institutions must ensure that AI policies do not create barriers to learning for students with disabilities. Where AI is recommended or required, alternatives must be available. Institutions should proactively consider how AI tools can enhance accessibility through features such as text to - speech, transcription, and translation. To make a product more accessible, educational institutions should solicit feedback from various end - user groups .
F . Explainability Generative AI Tools/systems explain the capacity for them to provide understandable explanations of their decisions and actions.
However, w hen AI systems are used to make decisions affecting students (such as automated assessment, plagiarism detection, or admissions sc reening), institutions must be able to explain how the AI system reached its conclusions. Students should have access to information about factors that influenced AI - based decisions about them.

8. Constructive and Encouraged Uses
This section identifies uses of generative AI that should be encouraged and supported as they enhance teaching, learning, and research. Institutions should actively promote these uses through professional development, infrastructure support, and policy clarity.
8 A . Teaching a nd Learning Applications
Faculty are encouraged to use generative AI in teaching contexts such as: (i) possibility engine — generating multiple examples, case studies, or problem variations to illustrate concepts; (ii) Socratic opponent — providing counterarguments or alternative pers pectives to deepen student thinking; (iii) collaboration coach — offering feedback on group dynamics, presentation skills, or project management; (iv) personal tutor — explaining concepts in different ways tailored to individual student needs; (v) co - designer — assisting in course design, learning objectives, and assessment alignment; (vi) study buddy — generating practice problems, study guides, or quiz questions for student self - study; (vii) motivator — providing encouragement and learning strategies during challen ging coursework; (viii) writing coach — offering feedback on clarity, organization, and argumentation in student drafts; and (ix) research assistant — supporting literature review, data synthesis, and research methodology planning .
Faculty should share pract ices and lessons learned through faculty development programs and departmental colloquia to build institutional knowledge and help colleagues implement these tools effectively 8B . Research Applications Researchers are encouraged to use generative AI to s upport research processes including: (i) literature review — identifying relevant studies, synthesizing findings, and mapping research landscapes; (ii) hypothesis generation — brainstorming research questions and theoretical frameworks; (iii) data analysis — ex ploring patterns, generating initial interpretations, and suggesting statistical approaches (subject to verification); (iv) writing improvement — enhancing clarity and coherence of research manuscripts; (v) translation — making research accessible across langu ages; (vi) citation verification — checking accuracy of citations and references; and (vii) replication checking — identifying potential methodological issues or alternative explanations.
Generative AI should never be listed as a research author. The researche r bears full responsibility for all content and claims in publications regardless of AI assistance used. AI use in research should be disclosed in appropriate sections (methods, acknowledgments) of published work and should comply with journal guidelines.
8 C . Administrative Applications
Administrative staff and managers are encouraged to use generative AI for: (i) translation — providing multilingual support for communications; (ii) document processing — organizing, summarizing, and categorizing administrativ e documents; (iii) communication drafting — preparing routine correspondence, announcements, and reports; and (iv) data management — assisting with data organization and basic analysis. Administrative use should comply with data privacy requirements and instit utional information security policies .

9. Data Sovereignty and Protection
Data sovereignty — the right of institutions to maintain control over institutional and student data — is a critical consideration in AI tool selection and deployment. Many cloud - based AI services process data on servers outside Pakistan or within jurisdictions with different data protection standards. Institutions should be aware of data flows and make informed choices about tools and data types. Sensitive institutional data (including student records, financial information, research data from government projects) should be processed using enterprise - grade AI tools with data residency guarantees or local hosting options. Publicly available or anonymized data may be processed using commer cial AI services, subject to explicit consent and compliance policies.
Institutions should consider deployment of local AI solutions where feasible, including open source language models and institutional AI services with on - premises hosting. These soluti ons provide greater data control and align with national data sovereignty objectives. Anonymization techniques — removing identifying information before processing — should be employed where sensitive data use cannot be avoided with commercial services.
The HEC will conduct a feasibility study within 12 months of framework adoption examining: (i) national AI infrastructure capacity; (ii) options for institutional or sectoral AI platforms with data residency in Pakistan; (iii) open - source AI model deployment i n HEIs; and (iv) procurement guidance for enterprise AI tools with appropriate data sovereignty provisions.

10. Rules and Penalties of Plagiarism
Academic integrity is something students really need to be aware of , and how their institution stands about it . When in doubt, make sure they know what is going on , but also contact teachers or have a meeting with their academic advisor. The use of generative AI tools such as ChatGPT helps enhance learning, particularly outside the academic setting (e.g. brainstor ming ideas, exploring concepts, etc.) while its use to complete assignments and Thesis will have very serious consequences. However, the information that these tools produce was originally written and published , and it may be incorrect or out of date . Plag iarism occurs when students use text generators without proper citation. The information supplied by these tools may be deficient as well as mistaken , which necessitates students to verify their work . The University expects students to present original wor k that they produced independently in all graded assignments and theses.
Academic misconduct , together with a zero grade , follows both plagiarism detection and unauthorized tool usage of Generative AI in student work.
The following rules shall be incorporated at the institutional level:
i. Author credit must not go to generative AI tools in any writing project. The usage of these
tools should remain limited to make texts more readable but safety precautions should always be deployed. O ne must include a declaration statement regarding all AI - assisted technologies or generative AI tool use before references in the final manuscript section.
Declaration of G enerative AI tools and AI - assisted technologies in the writing process:
“During the preparation of this work , the author(s) used [NAME TOOL / SERVICE] in order to [REASON]. After using this tool/service, the author(s) reviewed and edited the content as needed and take(s) full responsibility (legal, moral, etc.) for the content of the publication.”
ii. Universities need to create internal protocols that establish how students should acknowledge
the integration of Generative AI tools when preparing academic papers. Any content produced by AI systems needs to receive appropriate acknowledgment in addition to proper recognition of the original authors who provided referenced material.
iii. Generative AI tools have specific conditions where their academic implementation meets
approval according to Section 6. Educational researchers should not rely on Generative AI tools for collecting data because they yield inadequate information that includ es outmoded facts as well as spurious references and artificial research findings. Students must avoid all activities of data generation and fabrication , including data manipulation that utilizes Generative AI tools. The student will face responsibility fo r misconduct when this situation occurs.
iv. Plagiarism detection software, including tools capable of identifying AI - generated text, may
be used to assess content originality when available. The evaluation of similarity and acceptable use of AI - assisted cont ent shall be guided by the prevailing policies and guidelines issued by the HEC, with final determinations made by the DSC and the relevant statutory bodies of the respective HEIs. HEC periodically updates these guidelines in line with global best practice s and feedback from the academic community. Detection as a single indicator is insufficient for disciplinary action; detection must be accompanied by additional corroborating evidence of intent to misrepresent.
v. Aligned with the recent plagiarism policy of HEC
, the student of UG as well as PG must add the updated plagiarism undertaking statement stating:
“ I, [Name of Student] , declare on oath that the research work presented in my UG/MS/PhD project/thesis, where applicable, entitled “[Project/Thesis Title]” is my own research work and is not substantially contributed by any other person or Generative AI Tools. Any small contribution/assistance wherever received has been duly acknowledged/cited , and the whole project/thesis has been written by me under the latest plagiarism policy declared by HEC and [Name of University] , as per the policy on use of Generative AI Tools.
Where Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) tools have been used during the preparation of this project/thesis, such use has been limited to purposes permitted under the applicable HEC and University policies and guidelines. Any use of GenAI that has materially contributed to th e content, analysis, or presentation of this work has been appropriately acknowledged or disclosed, where required.
I further declare that GenAI tools have not been used to fabricate or falsify research data, results, references, citations, or sources, no r to generate work that is presented as my own in violation of applicable academic integrity requirements.
I understand that the use of GenAI does not relieve me of responsibility for the accuracy, originality, authenticity, proper citation, and academic integrity of this project/thesis. Any violation identified in this work shall be dealt with in accordance with the applicable policies and procedures of HEC and [Name of University]. ” The above undertaking statement s might be updated by HEC from time to time.
Policy Reference: ANTI - PLAGIARISM POLICY by HEC
vi. Ensure that the use of AI Generative Tools complies with existing copyright and intellectual
property laws. Universities should internally develop /implement guidelines that prevent the generation of content that infringes upon copyright, trademarks, or an y other legal rights (IPO, Ministry of Commerce, is a relevant government body in Pakistan) .
The project / Thesis shall be checked by the supervisor according to the rules mentioned above.
However, in case of any criteria under the rules that are not met , the supervisor may ask the student to revise the project/thesis report and resubmit it to the supervisor. If the revised project/thesis does not meet the criteria set under the rules, the student sha ll be held responsible, and the case sha ll be referred to the UMCC/ DSC or any other relevant committee of the department for awarding a penalty depending upon the level of severity. The participation of an AI - relevant member should also be mandatory in the constitution of these committees to ensure that AI - related concerns are properly addressed. If the departmental committee agrees, the case can be raised to respective university lev el committee and would be dealt with according to the rules and regulation s of the University/DAI .

Appendix 2 Responsibilities of HEIs and Organizations Promoting responsible GenAI Tools use in HEIs involves a combination of proactive measures, policy implementation, and educational initiatives.
i. Ethical Guidelines : Instructors/Supervisors should develop clear and comprehensive
guidelines specifically addressing the use of GenAI Too ls within the academic setting – how and when these tools can be used and when they cannot. These guidelines should outline acceptable use, potential risks, and consequences of misuse and must be referred/mentioned clearly in the course outlines and projec t proposal documents.
ii. Continuous Ethical Review : Create a AI Stewardship committee or ethical review board to
periodically assess GenAI Tools' use and address emerging ethical concerns related to AI technologies. Continuously assess the research manuscripts by field specialists for unethical use of GenAI Tools in light of relevant policies at the national level
iii. Connecting GenAI Tools Use to Learn ing Outcomes : At the outset of course, teachers /
supervisors should connect the use of AI Generative Tools to Outcome - Based Education (OBE)mapping it with learning outcomes (Program Learning Outcomes – PLOs & Course Learning Outcomes - CLOs). This helps students to understand how these tools can support their learning and what the guidelines/expectations are for them.
iv. Transparent Attribution : Encourage students and researchers to transparently attribute the
use of AI - generated content in their work, ensur ing proper acknowledgment and citation.
v. Faculty and Staff Capacity Building : Arrange awareness seminars and workshops to
faculty, staff, and administrators on the responsible and ethical use of GenAI Tools. Educators should understand the capabilities and limitations of GenAI Tools and be equipped to guide students appropriately. Emphasize the importance of academic honesty and integrity through these orientation programs, research seminars, and ongoing awareness campaigns. The universities can include the module related to Gen AI in the courses like “Introduction to ICT” (for BE/BS) or “Research Methodology” for (MS/PhD) , as these subjects are offered in all disciplines.
vi. Incorporate AI Ethics into Curriculum : Integrate discussions on AI ethics, bias, and
r esponsible AI use into relevant courses across various disciplines, including computer science, engineering, management , and social sciences.
vii. Critical Thinking and Information Literacy: Promote critical thinking skills among
students and researchers to hel p them critically evaluate AI - generated content and distinguish it from credible, validated sources.
viii. Originality and Creativity: Encourage and celebrate originality and creativity in student
work, discouraging the overreliance on AI - generated content for a ssignments and research.
ix. Collaboration with AI Developers: Establish collaborations with AI developers and
researchers to explore the ethical implications of AI and work together on responsible AI initiatives.
