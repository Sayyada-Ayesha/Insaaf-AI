[SECTION: FIR_PROCEDURE]
FIR (First Information Report) Filing Procedure:
1. Go to the police station that has jurisdiction over the area where the offence occurred.
2. Provide information about the cognizable offence to the Station House Officer (SHO) or officer in charge.
3. The police officer is LEGALLY BOUND to record your FIR (Section 154 CrPC). Police cannot refuse.
4. The information will be reduced to writing and read over to you.
5. You must sign the FIR after verification.
6. You are entitled to a free copy of the FIR.
7. The police will then begin investigation.
Required Documents: CNIC (national identity card), any evidence available.
Source: Code of Criminal Procedure, 1898, Section 154.
Source URL: https://pakistancode.gov.pk/

[SECTION: FIR_REFUSAL_REMEDY]
What To Do If Police Refuse To Register FIR:
If the police refuse to register your FIR, you have several legal remedies:
1. Written Application: Submit a written application to the SHO requesting FIR registration. Keep a copy.
2. SP/DSP Complaint: If the SHO refuses, file a written complaint to the Superintendent of Police (SP) or Deputy Superintendent of Police (DSP) of the area.
3. Ex-Officio Justice of Peace (Section 22-A/22-B CrPC): File an application before the Sessions Judge sitting as Ex-Officio Justice of Peace. The Sessions Judge can direct the SHO to register the FIR.
4. Direct Complaint to Magistrate (Section 200 CrPC): File a private complaint directly before the Magistrate.
5. High Court (Article 199): In extreme cases, file a writ petition before the High Court.
6. Pakistan Citizen Portal: File a complaint through the Pakistan Citizen Portal (Helpline 8171).
7. IGP Complaint Cell: Contact the Inspector General of Police complaint cell.
Source: CrPC Sections 22-A, 22-B, 154, 200; Constitution Article 199.

[SECTION: BAIL_PROCEDURE]
Bail Procedure in Pakistan:
There are three main types of bail:

A. Bail in Bailable Offences (Section 496 CrPC):
- This is a matter of RIGHT. The police or court MUST grant bail.
- The accused executes a bail bond with or without sureties.

B. Bail in Non-Bailable Offences (Section 497 CrPC):
- Not a matter of right; it is at the discretion of the court.
- Application is filed before the Sessions Court or High Court.
- Court considers: nature of offence, evidence, likelihood of fleeing, criminal record.
- Bail may be refused if accused is charged with offence punishable by death or life imprisonment and there are reasonable grounds to believe guilt.

C. Pre-Arrest Bail / Anticipatory Bail (Section 498-A CrPC):
- Filed BEFORE arrest when there is apprehension of arrest.
- Application filed before the Sessions Court or High Court.
- Court grants interim pre-arrest bail and issues notice to prosecution.
- After hearing both sides, court either confirms or recalls the bail.

Required Documents: Copy of FIR, surety bonds, CNIC, any supporting documents.
Costs: Court fee for bail application is minimal (stamp paper). Lawyer fees vary.
Source: CrPC Sections 496, 497, 498-A.
Source URL: https://pakistancode.gov.pk/

[SECTION: WRIT_PETITION_PROCEDURE]
Writ Petition Procedure (Article 199 of Constitution):
1. File writ petition before the High Court of the province where the cause of action arose.
2. Types of writs: Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Certiorari, Prohibition, Quo Warranto.
3. Petition must state fundamental right violated or illegal action by government/authority.
4. Attach supporting documents and affidavit.
5. Court may issue interim orders.
6. Notice is issued to respondent (government/authority).
7. Arguments are heard from both sides.
8. Court passes final order.
Required: Written petition, court fee (varies by High Court), supporting documents, affidavit.
Source: Constitution of Pakistan, Article 199; High Court Rules.

[SECTION: CRIMINAL_TRIAL_STAGES]
Criminal Trial Stages in Pakistan:
1. FIR Registration (Section 154 CrPC)
2. Investigation by Police (Section 156-173 CrPC)
3. Challan (Police Report) submitted to Magistrate (Section 173)
4. Framing of Charge (Section 242/265-C CrPC)
5. Plea of Accused (Guilty/Not Guilty)
6. Prosecution Evidence (examination-in-chief, cross-examination)
7. Statement of Accused under Section 342 CrPC
8. Defence Evidence (if accused wishes to produce)
9. Final Arguments
10. Judgment (conviction or acquittal)
11. Sentencing (if convicted)
12. Appeal to Higher Court (within 30 days)
Source: Code of Criminal Procedure, 1898.

[SECTION: CIVIL_SUIT_STAGES]
Civil Suit Stages in Pakistan:
1. Filing of Plaint (suit initiation) with court fee
2. Service of Summons to defendant
3. Written Statement by defendant (within 30 days)
4. Replication by plaintiff (if needed)
5. Framing of Issues
6. Documentary Evidence filing
7. Oral Evidence (examination of witnesses)
8. Final Arguments
9. Judgment and Decree
10. Execution of Decree (if needed)
11. Appeal to Appellate Court (within 30 days)
12. Second Appeal to High Court on question of law
13. Revision (Section 115 CPC)
Source: Code of Civil Procedure, 1908.

[SECTION: FAMILY_CASE_PROCEDURE]
Family Court Case Procedure:
1. Filing of suit in Family Court by wife/husband.
2. Court issues notice to opposite party.
3. Written Statement within 30 days.
4. Pre-trial/Reconciliation hearing (mandatory).
5. If reconciliation fails, case proceeds to trial.
6. Evidence from both parties.
7. Final arguments.
8. Judgment by Family Court.
9. Appeal to High Court within 30 days.
Types of family cases: dissolution of marriage (Khula/divorce), maintenance, custody, dower recovery, restitution of conjugal rights, dowry recovery.
Source: West Pakistan Family Courts Act, 1964.

[SECTION: CYBER_COMPLAINT_PROCEDURE]
Cybercrime Complaint Procedure:
1. Register complaint online at https://complaint.fia.gov.pk/ OR call FIA Helpline 1991.
2. Visit nearest FIA Cybercrime Circle Office with CNIC.
3. Provide written complaint describing the offence.
4. Attach evidence: screenshots, URLs, messages, call records, emails.
5. FIA registers complaint and assigns investigation officer.
6. FIA may summon the accused and investigate.
7. If evidence is sufficient, FIA files challan in Cybercrime Court.
8. Trial proceeds in the designated Cybercrime Court.
Required Documents: CNIC, written complaint, evidence (screenshots, URLs).
Source: PECA 2016; FIA website.
Source URL: https://complaint.fia.gov.pk/

[SECTION: CONSUMER_COMPLAINT_STEPS]
Consumer Court Complaint Steps:
1. Send Legal Notice (15 days) to seller/manufacturer.
2. If not resolved, file complaint in District Consumer Court.
3. No court fee required.
4. Attach receipt, warranty, evidence of defect.
5. Court issues notice to seller/manufacturer.
6. Evidence and arguments.
7. Court order: refund, replacement, or compensation.
8. Appeal within 30 days.
Source: Provincial Consumer Protection Acts.

[SECTION: NIKAH_NAMA_REGISTRATION]
Nikah Nama (Marriage Certificate) Registration:
1. Marriage is performed by a licensed Nikah Registrar (Nikah Khwan).
2. Nikah Nama (marriage contract) is filled out with details of bride, groom, witnesses, and dower (Mehr).
3. The Nikah Registrar submits the Nikah Nama to the Union Council within 30 days.
4. Union Council registers the marriage and issues a certified copy.
5. The marriage certificate can be used for CNIC update, passport, visa applications.
6. Failure to register a Nikah is an offence under Muslim Family Laws Ordinance, 1961.
Required Documents: CNICs of bride and groom, CNICs of witnesses.
Source: Muslim Family Laws Ordinance, 1961, Section 5.
