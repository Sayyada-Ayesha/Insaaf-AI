[SECTION: 1] Police Order, 2002 (Chief Executive's Order No. 22 of 2002) — Reorganizes policing in Pakistan, replacing the Police Act 1861. Source: Pakistan Code (pakistancode.gov.pk). Status: CURRENT (provincial adaptations may vary).

[SECTION: 2] Fundamental principles — The police shall be a professional, impartial and service-oriented organization. Police functions include: (a) protection of life, liberty and property; (b) prevention and detection of crime; (c) prosecution of offenders; (d) regulation of public assemblies; (e) traffic management.

[SECTION: 3] Duties of police — Every police officer shall: (a) prevent the commission of offences; (b) collect intelligence affecting public peace; (c) register FIR and investigate cognizable offences; (d) arrest offenders; (e) execute warrants; (f) provide assistance in natural disasters; (g) regulate traffic; (h) assist the public in emergencies.

[SECTION: 4] Police stations and jurisdiction — A police station is the basic unit of policing. Each police station has territorial jurisdiction. The Station House Officer (SHO) is in charge. Each police station maintains: station diary, daily diary (Roznamcha), FIR register, case diaries, and property register.

[SECTION: 5] Station House Officer (SHO) — The SHO has overall responsibility for the police station including supervision of investigation, maintenance of law and order, and public service. The SHO must be present at the station or available on communication at all times.

[SECTION: 6] Registration of FIR — Section 154 CrPC read with Police Order: The police MUST register FIR for cognizable offences. Refusal to register is a dereliction of duty. Remedies for FIR refusal: (a) complaint to Deputy Superintendent of Police; (b) application to Sessions Judge/Judicial Magistrate under Section 22-A/22-B CrPC; (c) writ petition under Article 199.

[SECTION: 7] Investigation — Investigation includes: visiting the crime scene, collecting evidence, recording statements (Section 161 CrPC), conducting searches, arresting suspects, and preparing charge sheet (challan). Investigation must be completed within 14 days for most offences; extendable up to 90 days with court permission.

[SECTION: 8] Public Safety Commissions — Public Safety Commissions at federal, provincial and district levels oversee police performance and ensure independence from political influence. Commissions review complaints and make recommendations.

[SECTION: 9] Police complaints authority — Citizens may file complaints against police officers for: (a) refusal to register FIR; (b) illegal detention; (c) torture or abuse; (d) corruption; (e) misuse of authority. Complaints may be filed with the District Public Safety Commission or the Provincial Police Officer.

[SECTION: 10] Police reforms — Various provinces have enacted their own police laws: (a) Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Police Act 2017; (b) Sindh Police Act 2020; (c) Punjab Police Reforms (ongoing). These provide for: separate investigation wings, community policing, women police stations, and independent complaint mechanisms.

[SECTION: 11] Women police stations — Women police stations exist in major cities. Women police officers handle complaints from women. Staff are specially trained in gender-sensitive investigation and victim support.

[SECTION: 12] Community policing — SHO and police officers are encouraged to engage with local communities through citizen liaison committees, community forums and police-community partnerships.

[SECTION: 13] Missing persons — Police must promptly register FIR for missing persons. Investigation must include tracing through mobile data, CCTV footage, hospitals, jails and morgues. Quarterly progress reports must be submitted to the High Court.
