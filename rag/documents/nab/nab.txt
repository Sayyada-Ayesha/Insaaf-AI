[SECTION: 1] National Accountability Bureau Ordinance, 1999 (Ordinance No. XVIII of 1999) — The primary anti-corruption law in Pakistan. Establishes the National Accountability Bureau (NAB). Source: Pakistan Code (pakistancode.gov.pk). Status: CURRENT.

[SECTION: 2] Definitions — 'Holder of public office' includes Presidents, Prime Ministers, federal/provincial ministers, MNAs, MPAs, judges, public servants, government employees, and employees of state-owned corporations. 'Corrupt practice' and 'corruption' defined broadly.

[SECTION: 3] Establishment of NAB — NAB is established as the primary anti-corruption agency. The Chairman is appointed by the President in consultation with the Leader of the House and the Leader of the Opposition in the National Assembly.

[SECTION: 4] NAB Chairman — The Chairman holds office for a term of 4 years and is eligible for reappointment. The Chairman may not be removed except on grounds of physical/mental incapacity or misconduct.

[SECTION: 5] NAB Prosecutor General — The Prosecutor General is appointed by the President on the advice of the Chairman NAB. He is assisted by Deputy Prosecutors General.

[SECTION: 6] Offences of corruption — The following are offences: (a) bribery (offering or accepting gratification); (b) criminal breach of trust; (c) cheating, fraud and misappropriation; (d) misuse of authority; (e) assets disproportionate to known sources of income; (f) willful default in payment of government dues.

[SECTION: 7] Graft, bribery — A holder of public office who solicits or accepts any gratification as a motive or reward for doing any official act commits an offence. Punishment: imprisonment up to 14 years and fine.

[SECTION: 8] Criminal breach of trust — A holder of public office who commits criminal breach of trust in respect of public property commits an offence. Punishment: imprisonment up to 14 years and fine.

[SECTION: 9] Cheating, fraud — Fraudulent acquisition of public property or funds. Punishment: imprisonment up to 14 years and fine.

[SECTION: 10] Misuse of authority — Wilful misuse of authority causing loss to the public or gain to any person. Punishment: imprisonment up to 10 years and fine.

[SECTION: 11] Assets disproportionate to income — A holder of public office or any other person who holds assets disproportionate to known sources of income commits an offence. Punishment: imprisonment up to 14 years and fine. Property acquired through corruption may be forfeited.

[SECTION: 12] Willful default — Willful default in payment of tax, duty or other government dues. Punishment: imprisonment up to 5 years and fine.

[SECTION: 13] Punishment for abetment — Whoever abets the commission of an offence under this Ordinance shall be punished with the same punishment as the principal offender.

[SECTION: 14] Investigation — NAB may investigate any alleged offence. The Chairman may authorize investigation. NAB has powers to arrest, search, seize, and freeze assets.

[SECTION: 15] Arrest and bail — NAB may arrest without warrant. Bail is not a matter of right for NAB offences. The court may grant bail only if there are reasonable grounds to believe the accused is not guilty and unlikely to commit further offences.

[SECTION: 16] Trial — Offences under this Ordinance are tried by the Accountability Court, established by the federal government. The court has exclusive jurisdiction. Trial must be concluded within 30 days, extendable up to 90 days.

[SECTION: 17] Burden of proof — Where any property is alleged to be disproportionate to known sources of income, the burden of proving the property was lawfully acquired lies on the accused.

[SECTION: 18] Plea bargain — An accused may enter into a plea bargain with NAB, agreeing to return the assets/proceeds of corruption. Upon return, the court may release the accused on such terms as deemed appropriate.

[SECTION: 19] Appeal — Appeals from the Accountability Court lie to the High Court within 30 days. Further appeal to the Supreme Court within 30 days.

[SECTION: 20] Voluntary return — A person who has obtained property through corrupt practices may voluntarily return it to NAB before commencement of trial. NAB may close the inquiry.

[SECTION: 21] Protection of witnesses — The court may keep the identity of witnesses confidential, hold in-camera proceedings, and take measures to protect witnesses.

[SECTION: 22] Review of NAB amendments — The Supreme Court of Pakistan has reviewed certain provisions of NAB law. Key amendments by Parliament on various occasions have been subject to judicial scrutiny. Current law reflects amendments.
