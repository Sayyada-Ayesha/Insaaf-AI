Consumer Rights in Pakistan
Consumers in Pakistan are entitled to the following rights under provincial consumer protection legislation:
1. Right to Safety: Protection from hazardous goods and services that are detrimental to health and safety.
2. Right to Information: Right to be informed about quality, quantity, potency, purity, standard and price of goods or services.
3. Right to Choice: Right to choose from a variety of goods and services at competitive prices.
4. Right to Redressal: Right to seek redressal against unfair trade practices, restrictive trade practices, and exploitation.
5. Right to Consumer Education: Right to be educated about consumer rights and responsibilities.
Source: Punjab Consumer Protection Act, 2005; Sindh Consumer Protection Act, 2014; KPK Consumer Protection Act, 1997; Balochistan Consumer Protection Act, 2003.

[SECTION: PUNJAB_CONSUMER_PROTECTION_ACT]
Punjab Consumer Protection Act, 2005 — Key Provisions
Section 2(c): "Consumer" means any person who buys goods or avails of services for a consideration, but not for commercial resale.
Section 3: Rights of consumers as enumerated above.
Section 8: Establishment of Consumer Courts at the District level.
Section 9: Jurisdiction of Consumer Court — complaints where goods/services value does not exceed the prescribed limit (as amended).
Section 12: Power of Consumer Court to call for information.
Section 15: Any consumer or registered consumer association may file a complaint.
Section 16: Complaint Procedure — complaint filed in writing, court issues notice to opposite party, parties heard, inspection may be ordered.
Section 17: Relief available to complainant:
  - Removal of defect in goods.
  - Replacement of goods.
  - Refund of price paid.
  - Compensation for any loss or injury suffered.
  - Discontinuation of unfair trade practice.
  - Award of costs.
Section 18: Appeal — Any party aggrieved by the order of Consumer Court may appeal to the Consumer Appellate Court within 30 days.
No court fee is required for filing a consumer complaint.
Complaint must be filed within 30 days of purchase or discovery of defect (limitation period).
Source: Punjab Consumer Protection Act, 2005; Punjab Gazette.

[SECTION: SINDH_CONSUMER_PROTECTION_ACT]
Sindh Consumer Protection Act, 2014 — Key Provisions
The Sindh Consumer Protection Act, 2014 established the Sindh Consumer Protection Council and Consumer Courts in Sindh.
Key features:
- Establishes District Consumer Courts and Consumer Appellate Courts.
- Covers both goods and services.
- Provides for compensation, refund, replacement, and award of costs.
- Complaint must be filed within 90 days of cause of action.
- No court fee for consumer complaints.
- Consumer Protection Council advises Government on consumer matters.
Source: Sindh Consumer Protection Act, 2014; Sindh Gazette.

[SECTION: CONSUMER_COMPLAINT_PROCEDURE]
Consumer Complaint Procedure — Step-by-Step
Step 1 — Send Legal Notice: Before filing a consumer court case, send a mandatory 15-day legal notice to the manufacturer, seller, or service provider in writing. State the defect/issue and the relief sought.
Step 2 — No Response or Unsatisfactory Response: If no satisfactory response is received within 15 days, proceed to file a complaint.
Step 3 — File Complaint in District Consumer Court: Prepare a written complaint including:
  - Name, address, and contact of complainant.
  - Name and address of the opposite party (seller/manufacturer/service provider).
  - Description of goods/services purchased and defect/issue.
  - Details of legal notice and response (if any).
  - Relief sought.
  - Attach: Receipt/invoice, warranty card, photographs of defective product, copy of legal notice, any correspondence.
Step 4 — Consumer Court Issues Notice: The Court registers the complaint and issues notice to the opposite party.
Step 5 — Reply by Opposite Party: The opposite party must file a written reply.
Step 6 — Hearing and Evidence: Both parties present their evidence. Court may order inspection of the defective goods/premises.
Step 7 — Order: Consumer Court passes a reasoned order — refund, replacement, compensation, or dismissal.
Step 8 — Appeal: Any aggrieved party may appeal to the Consumer Appellate Court within 30 days of the order.
No court fee is payable at any stage for consumer complaints.
Source: Provincial Consumer Protection Acts; Punjab Consumer Protection Act, 2005.

Defective Goods and Products Liability
- Any trader or manufacturer who sells a defective product is liable under consumer protection law.
- Defect includes: manufacturing defect, design defect, inadequate warnings/instructions.
- The warranty period stated at the time of sale must be honored.
- If a product causes injury or damage due to a defect, the manufacturer/seller is liable for compensation.
- Misleading advertisements that induce purchase constitute unfair trade practice and are actionable.
- After-sale service promised at the time of sale must be provided.
- Sale of goods not conforming to declared standards (adulterated food, substandard medicines, etc.) is also actionable under other sector-specific laws.
Source: Provincial Consumer Protection Acts; Sale of Goods Act, 1930; Punjab Pure Food Act; Pakistan Standards and Quality Control Authority (PSQCA) Act.

[SECTION: UNFAIR_TRADE_PRACTICES]
Unfair Trade Practices
The following are prohibited unfair trade practices under Pakistani consumer law:
- False representation of goods or services.
- False representation that goods are new or of a particular grade.
- False representation of sponsorship, approval, or affiliation.
- Misleading price claims (e.g., false "sale" prices).
- Offering prizes/gifts with intent not to provide them.
- Misleading advertising.
- Withholding relevant information about goods or services from a consumer.
Consumers may file complaints in Consumer Court against unfair trade practices.
Source: Punjab Consumer Protection Act, 2005; Sindh Consumer Protection Act, 2014.

[SECTION: PSQCA_STANDARDS]
Pakistan Standards and Quality Control Authority (PSQCA)
PSQCA is the national body responsible for standardization and quality control of goods in Pakistan.
- PSQCA issues Pakistani Standards (PS) for goods.
- Mandatory standards apply to specified categories of goods.
- Goods not meeting mandatory standards cannot be legally sold in Pakistan.
- PSQCA can conduct inspections and seize non-conforming goods.
- Complaints about substandard goods can be filed with PSQCA.
Contact: PSQCA Helpline, psqca.com.pk
Source: Pakistan Standards and Quality Control Authority Act, 1996.
