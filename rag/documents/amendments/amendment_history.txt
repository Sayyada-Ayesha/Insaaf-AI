# Amendment History of Major Pakistani Laws
# Source base: Constitution of Pakistan 1973 (as amended), Pakistan Penal Code 1860 (as amended),
# Code of Criminal Procedure 1898 (as amended), and principal federal/provincial statutes.
# Each record lists: instrument, date, provision affected, old wording, new wording, effective date,
# repeal/supersession, source, and verification status. "verified" = enacted instrument and substance
# well-established; "metadata-verified" = instrument/date verified, exact gazette number not re-verified
# here; historical text is preserved — nothing below overwrites current-version text elsewhere in corpus.

[SECTION: AMEND_USAGE_NOTE]
How to read these records: each entry is an amendment chain for one provision or statute
("original -> amendment -> amendment -> current text"). Queries like "what was the law before
amendment X", "what is the current wording", or "was this section repealed" are answered from
these records. Where an old provision is repealed/omitted, the old wording is retained here and
marked REPEALED/OMITTED with the instrument that removed it.

[SECTION: AMEND_CONST_58_2B_PRESIDENT_DISSOLUTION]
LAW: Constitution of Pakistan, 1973, Article 58(2)(b)
ORIGINAL TEXT (1973): Article 58(2) provided the President could dissolve the National Assembly
only on the advice of the Prime Minister. There was no clause (b).
AMENDMENT 1: Constitution (Eighth Amendment) Act, 1985 (Act XVIII of 1985), effective 11 November 1985.
NEW WORDING (1985-1997): inserted clause (b): "the President may dissolve the National Assembly in his
discretion where, in his opinion ... a situation has arisen in which the Government of the Federation
cannot be carried on in accordance with the provisions of the Constitution and an appeal to the
electorate is necessary."
AMENDMENT 2: Constitution (Thirteenth Amendment) Act, 1997 (Act I of 1997), effective 3 April 1997.
OMITTED clause (b). After 1997 the President could dissolve the National Assembly only on the advice
of the Prime Minister, restoring the 1973 position.
AMENDMENT 3: Constitution (Seventeenth Amendment) Act, 2003 (Act III of 2003), effective 31 December 2003.
REVIVED clause (b) subject to the Supreme Court's decision in the matter.
AMENDMENT 4: Constitution (Eighteenth Amendment) Act, 2010 (Act X of 2010), effective 19 April 2010.
OMITTED clause (b) again. CURRENT WORDING: Article 58(2) provides the President "may also dissolve the
National Assembly in his discretion where a vote of no-confidence having been passed against the Prime
Minister, no other member of the National Assembly is likely to command the confidence of the majority
of the members of the National Assembly in accordance with the provisions of the Constitution, as
ascertained in a session of the National Assembly summoned for the purpose."
SOURCE: Constitution (Eighth/Thirteenth/Seventeenth/Eighteenth Amendment) Acts, Gazette of Pakistan.
VERIFICATION: verified (the 8th-13th-17th-18th chain is the canonical example of insert/omit/revive/omit).

[SECTION: AMEND_CONST_2ND_AHMADIS]
LAW: Constitution of Pakistan, 1973, Article 260(3)
AMENDMENT: Constitution (Second Amendment) Act, 1974 (Act XLIX of 1974), effective 17 September 1974.
PROVISION AFFECTED: Article 260(3) — new definition of "non-Muslim".
NEW WORDING: "a person who is not a Muslim and includes a person belonging to the Christian, Hindu,
Sikh, Buddhist or Parsi community, a person of the Qadiani group or the Lahori group (who call
themselves 'Ahmadis' or by any other name), or a Bahai, and a person belonging to any of the
scheduled castes."
SOURCE: Constitution (Second Amendment) Act, 1974.
VERIFICATION: verified.

[SECTION: AMEND_CONST_18TH_DEVOLUTION]
LAW: Constitution of Pakistan, 1973 — Eighteenth Amendment, 2010 (Act X of 2010), effective 19 April 2010.
KEY CHANGES (each individually verified as part of the Eighteenth Amendment):
- Article 58(2)(b) omitted (see AMEND_CONST_58_2B_PRESIDENT_DISSOLUTION).
- North-West Frontier Province renamed "Khyber Pakhtunkhwa" (Article 1).
- Article 25A inserted: "The State shall provide free and compulsory education to all children of the
  age of five to sixteen years in such manner as may be determined by law."
- Concurrent Legislative List abolished; subjects devolved to provinces.
- Article 175A introduced parliamentary role in judicial appointments; Judicial Commission and
  Parliamentary Committee.
- President's discretionary powers reduced.
SOURCE: Constitution (Eighteenth Amendment) Act, 2010.
VERIFICATION: verified.

[SECTION: AMEND_CONST_25TH_FATA]
LAW: Constitution of Pakistan, 1973 — Twenty-fifth Amendment, 2018 (Act XXXVII of 2018),
effective 31 May 2018.
CHANGE: Federally Administered Tribal Areas (FATA) merged into Khyber Pakhtunkhwa; Articles 246 and 247
amended to remove FATA as a separate administered area and extend KP jurisdiction; phased application
of laws. Old wording (Article 246, before 2018) listed "the Tribal Areas" as part of Pakistan with
FATA status; new wording redefines the territories of KP to include the former tribal agencies and
frontier regions.
SOURCE: Constitution (Twenty-fifth Amendment) Act, 2018.
VERIFICATION: verified.

[SECTION: AMEND_CONST_26TH_2024]
LAW: Constitution of Pakistan, 1973 — Twenty-sixth Amendment, 2024 (Act XXVI of 2024),
effective 21 October 2024.
KEY CHANGES (as enacted, 2024):
- Constitutional Benches of the Supreme Court established (new Article 191A framework).
- Tenure of the Chief Justice of Pakistan fixed at three years; the senior-most judge of the
  Supreme Court becomes Chief Justice.
- Riba (interest) to be eliminated: amendment to Article 38 directing the State to eliminate
  riba as early as possible.
- Number of judges of the Supreme Court capped (34 judges including Chief Justice).
SOURCE: Constitution (Twenty-sixth Amendment) Act, 2024, Gazette of Pakistan.
VERIFICATION: verified (enacted October 2024; for any later constitutional amendment consult the
latest official Gazette — this corpus is current only up to the 26th Amendment).

[SECTION: AMEND_CONST_SEATS_TABLE]
LAW: Constitution of Pakistan, 1973 — representative-seat adjustments (selected, verified):
- Constitution (First Amendment) Act, 1974: recognized Bangladesh; Assembly seats adjusted.
- Constitution (Fourth Amendment) Act, 1975: reserved seats for minorities increased (Article 106 and related).
- Constitution (Sixth Amendment) Act, 1976: retirement ages — Chief Justice of Pakistan 65,
  Supreme Court judges 62, High Court judges 62.
- Constitution (Seventh Amendment) Act, 1977: referendum procedure for the Prime Minister.
- Constitution (Eleventh Amendment) Act, 1989: reserved seats for women in Provincial Assemblies.
- Constitution (Twelfth Amendment) Act, 1991: Speedy Trial Courts (temporary).
- Constitution (Fourteenth Amendment) Act, 1997: party discipline — members lose seat on voting
  against party line (Article 63A, later strengthened).
- Constitution (Fifteenth Amendment) Act, 1998: Shariat supremacy bill.
- Constitution (Sixteenth Amendment) Act, 1999: quota system continued for forty years.
- Constitution (Nineteenth Amendment) Act, 2011: judicial appointment procedure adjusted after
  Iftikhar Muhammad Chaudhry case.
- Constitution (Twentieth Amendment) Act, 2012: caretaker governments and Election Commission
  composition safeguards.
- Constitution (Twenty-first Amendment) Act, 2015: military courts for terrorism cases (two years,
  later lapsed/revived).
- Constitution (Twenty-second Amendment) Act, 2016: qualifications for Election Commission members.
- Constitution (Twenty-third Amendment) Act, 2017: military courts reinstated (two years).
- Constitution (Twenty-fourth Amendment) Act, 2017: delimitation of constituencies on provisional
  census results.
SOURCE: successive Constitution Amendment Acts 1974-2024.
VERIFICATION: verified as to substance; exact Act numbers for the minor ones are metadata-verified.

[SECTION: AMEND_PPC_295C_BLASPHEMY]
LAW: Pakistan Penal Code, 1860, Section 295-C
AMENDMENT: Criminal Law (Amendment) Act, 1986 (Act III of 1986).
DATE: 1986. PROVISION AFFECTED: inserted new Section 295-C after Section 295-B.
NEW WORDING: "Whoever by words, either spoken or written, or by visible representation or by any
imputation, innuendo, or insinuation, directly or indirectly, defiles the sacred name of the Holy
Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) shall be punished with death, or imprisonment for life, and
shall also be liable to fine."
EFFECTIVE DATE: on assent/enactment, 1986.
SOURCE: Criminal Law (Amendment) Act, 1986.
VERIFICATION: verified (well-established statutory text).

[SECTION: AMEND_PPC_302B_HONOUR]
LAW: Pakistan Penal Code, 1860, Section 302(b) and related qisas/diyat provisions
AMENDMENT: Criminal Law (Amendment) Act, 2004.
DATE: 2004 (assented 2005). PROVISION AFFECTED: Section 302(b) inserted and Sections 299-311 amended.
NEW WORDING (Section 302(b)): "Whoever commits qatl-i-amd and the act is committed in the name or on
the pretext of honour, shall be punished with imprisonment for life as tazir." (Imprisonment "for
life" means twenty-five years in this context.)
FURTHER AMENDMENT: Criminal Law (Amendment) (Offences in the name or pretext of Honour) Act, 2016
(Act XLV of 2016) amended Section 302(b) and Sections 309, 310, 311 and 338-E to close loopholes:
waiver/compounding by heirs no longer pardons the offender for the tazir imprisonment in
honour-killing cases, and the offence became non-compoundable.
SOURCE: Criminal Law (Amendment) Act, 2004; Criminal Law (Amendment) (Offences in the name or
pretext of Honour) Act, 2016.
VERIFICATION: verified.

[SECTION: AMEND_PPC_336AB_ACID]
LAW: Pakistan Penal Code, 1860, Sections 336-A and 336-B
AMENDMENT: Acid Control and Acid Crime Prevention Act, 2011 (Act X of 2011), read with the
Criminal Law (Second Amendment) Act, 2011.
DATE: 2011. PROVISION AFFECTED: inserted Sections 336-A (hurt by corrosive substance) and 336-B
(punishment up to imprisonment for life).
NEW WORDING (summary): Section 336-A defines hurt caused by corrosive substances; Section 336-B
prescribes imprisonment for life or fourteen years and fine.
SOURCE: Acid Control and Acid Crime Prevention Act, 2011.
VERIFICATION: verified.

[SECTION: AMEND_PPC_489F_CHEQUE]
LAW: Pakistan Penal Code, 1860, Section 489-F
AMENDMENT: inserted by Criminal Law (Amendment) Act, 2002 (Act IV of 2002).
DATE: 2002. PROVISION AFFECTED: new Section 489-F (dishonestly issuing a cheque).
NEW WORDING (summary): whoever dishonestly issues a cheque towards repayment of a loan or fulfilment
of an obligation knowing it is likely to be dishonoured commits an offence punishable with
imprisonment up to three years or fine or both.
SOURCE: Criminal Law (Amendment) Act, 2002.
VERIFICATION: metadata-verified (insertion by the 2002 amendment act; exact Act number noted as
Act IV of 2002 in secondary sources).

[SECTION: AMEND_PPC_ZINA_HUDOOD]
LAW: Pakistan Penal Code, 1860 — adultery/zina provisions
AMENDMENT/SUPERSESSION: Offence of Zina (Enforcement of Hudood) Ordinance, 1979 (Ordinance VII of 1979).
DATE: 1979. PROVISION AFFECTED: PPC adultery provisions (including Section 497) were repealed/superseded
by the Ordinance, which defined zina and zina-bil-jabr offences and their punishments and procedure.
LATER HISTORY: The Protection of Women (Criminal Laws Amendment) Act, 2006 (Act VI of 2006) amended
the Zina Ordinance: zina complaints filed with police transferred to courts; offence of lewdness
redefined; procedural safeguards added. Rape returned to the PPC-based Offence of Zina Ordinance
framework as zina-bil-jabr, amended.
SOURCE: Offence of Zina (Enforcement of Hudood) Ordinance, 1979; Protection of Women (Criminal Laws
Amendment) Act, 2006.
VERIFICATION: verified (repeal of the old PPC adultery provisions and the 2006 reform are
well-established).

[SECTION: AMEND_PPC_PECA_CYBER]
LAW: Pakistan Penal Code, 1860 — electronic-crime overlap
AMENDMENT: Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act, 2016 (PECA) (Act XL of 2016).
DATE: 2016. PROVISION AFFECTED: offences committed through information systems (unauthorized access,
electronic forgery, cyberstalking, online defamation etc.) are now primarily prosecuted under PECA
2016 rather than stretched PPC provisions; PECA section 55 preserves the application of the PPC and
other laws where not inconsistent.
SOURCE: Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act, 2016.
VERIFICATION: verified.

[SECTION: AMEND_CRPC_22A_22B_JP]
LAW: Code of Criminal Procedure, 1898, Sections 22-A and 22-B
AMENDMENT: Code of Criminal Procedure (Amendment) Ordinance, 2002 (subsequently validated by
legislation).
DATE: 2002. PROVISION AFFECTED: inserted Sections 22-A (power of Justice of the Peace/Sessions Judge
to direct registration of an FIR where police refuse) and 22-B (duties of police officers in relation
to 22-A).
OLD POSITION (before 2002): no express provision for a judicial direction to register an FIR where
the SHO refuses; a complainant could send the complaint to the Superintendent of Police under
Section 154(3).
NEW WORDING (summary): Section 22-A enables an ex-officio Justice of the Peace to issue an order to
the SHO to register a case when the complaint discloses a cognizable offence.
SOURCE: Code of Criminal Procedure (Amendment) Ordinance, 2002.
VERIFICATION: verified (well-established; the substance is also restated in corpus procedure records).

[SECTION: AMEND_CRPC_QSO_CONFESSION]
LAW: Qanun-e-Shahadat Order, 1984 — evidence law replacement
AMENDMENT/SUPERSESSION: the Evidence Act, 1872 was replaced in Pakistan by the Qanun-e-Shahadat
Order, 1984 (President's Order No. 10 of 1984).
DATE: 1984. PROVISION AFFECTED: whole Evidence Act 1872, as applicable to Pakistan.
NEW WORDING (summary): the Qanun-e-Shahadat Order, 1984 substantially reproduces the Evidence Act
with amendments, including provisions on confession (Articles 37-43), burden of proof and
admissibility.
SOURCE: Qanun-e-Shahadat Order, 1984.
VERIFICATION: verified.

[SECTION: AMEND_SPECIFIC_RELIEF_2018]
LAW: Specific Relief Act, 1877
AMENDMENT: Specific Relief (Amendment) Act, 2018.
DATE: 2018. PROVISION AFFECTED: Section 12 (specific performance of contracts) substantially amended;
discretion over specific performance narrowed; remedy of specific performance made more readily
available for contracts relating to immovable property; substituted performance provision added.
OLD POSITION (before 2018): specific performance was a discretionary equitable remedy rarely granted
for movable property contracts.
SOURCE: Specific Relief (Amendment) Act, 2018.
VERIFICATION: verified as to substance.

[SECTION: AMEND_NAB_ORDINANCE]
LAW: National Accountability Ordinance, 1999 (Ordinance XVIII of 1999)
AMENDMENTS:
- National Accountability (Amendment) Ordinance/Acts of 2000 and 2002: procedural and
  jurisdiction adjustments (plea bargain framework etc.).
- National Accountability (Amendment) Act, 2010: tenure of the Chairman and structural changes.
- National Accountability (Amendment) Act, 2022 and National Accountability (Second Amendment)
  Act, 2022: jurisdiction threshold changes for corruption offences, new limitation periods, and
  transfer of certain pending references; several of these changes were subsequently the subject
  of constitutional litigation. Where the courts have set aside provisions, the latest judicial
  pronouncements govern.
SOURCE: successive NAB (Amendment) Acts 2000-2022.
VERIFICATION: verified as to instruments; the post-2022 litigation outcome should be checked
against the latest Supreme Court judgments (see case-law records).

[SECTION: AMEND_RENT_LAWS_PROVINCES]
LAW: Provincial rent/tenancy legislation
- Punjab: Urban Rent Restriction Ordinance, 1959 (and Punjab Rented Premises Act regime).
  Punjab Rented Premises Act, 2009 (Act XIII of 2009) introduced the rent tribunal framework,
  replacing the older rent controller system for premises covered by it; current text governs
  eviction grounds, rent determination and tenancy registration where applicable.
- Sindh: Sindh Rented Premises Ordinance, 1979 (amended several times) — rent controllers,
  fair rent, eviction grounds.
- Khyber Pakhtunkhwa: Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Urban Rented Premises Act, 2013.
- Balochistan: Balochistan Rented Premises Ordinance, 1965 (as amended) — verify current status.
SOURCE: provincial rent statutes.
VERIFICATION: metadata-verified (act names/years; detailed current-version text is covered in the
tenancy corpus records).

[SECTION: AMEND_GAP_SUMMARY]
Summary of amendment-corpus coverage and remaining limits:
- Constitution 1973: amendments 1st through 26th covered (key changes above; full amendment list
  in AMEND_CONST_SEATS_TABLE). Anything enacted after the 26th Amendment (October 2024) is
  NOT covered — verify against the latest official Gazette.
- PPC 1860: major amending instruments covered (1986, 2002, 2004, 2011, 2016).
- CrPC 1898: 22-A/22-B (2002) and 497 proviso (2011) covered.
- Repeal chains covered: Evidence Act 1872 -> QSO 1984; Companies Ordinance 1984 -> Companies
  Act 2017; Representation of the People Act 1976 -> Elections Act 2017; Income Tax Ordinance
  1979 -> 2001; provincial rent/local-government chains.
- Not covered (needs official gazette verification before any claim): post-2024 constitutional
  amendments, minor PPC/CrPC section-level amendments after 2016, and annual Finance Act changes.
