[SECTION: 1] Fundamental Rights — Constitution of Pakistan 1973, Part II (Articles 7-28). Source: Constitution of Pakistan. Status: CURRENT.

[SECTION: 2] Article 9: Right to Life and Liberty — No person shall be deprived of life or liberty save in accordance with law. Courts have interpreted 'life' broadly to include: right to livelihood, clean environment, health, shelter, and dignity.

[SECTION: 3] Article 10: Safeguards as to Arrest — (1) Arrested person must be informed of grounds of arrest within 24 hours. (2) Right to consult and be defended by a legal practitioner. (3) Produced before magistrate within 24 hours. (4) Not detained beyond 24 hours without magistrate's authority. (5) No special preventive detention beyond 1 month without review board approval.

[SECTION: 4] Article 10A: Right to Fair Trial — Every citizen is entitled to a fair trial and due process for determination of civil rights and obligations or criminal charges. Includes: right to present evidence, cross-examine witnesses, impartial tribunal, and reasoned judgment.

[SECTION: 5] Article 11: Slavery, Forced Labour Abolished — Slavery and forced labour are prohibited. No child below 14 may be employed in hazardous employment. Bonded labour is a criminal offence.

[SECTION: 6] Article 12: Protection against Retrospective Punishment — No law shall authorize punishment for an act that was not an offence at the time of commission. No penalty greater than that applicable at the time of the offence.

[SECTION: 7] Article 13: Double Jeopardy — No person shall be prosecuted or punished for the same offence more than once.

[SECTION: 8] Article 14: Dignity and Privacy — The dignity of man is inviolable. No person shall be subjected to torture or cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment. Right to privacy of home and correspondence.

[SECTION: 9] Article 16: Freedom of Assembly — Citizens have the right to assemble peacefully and without arms, subject to reasonable restrictions imposed by law in the interest of public order.

[SECTION: 10] Article 17: Freedom of Association — Citizens have the right to form associations or unions, subject to law. Political parties must not be formed on sectarian/religious hate basis. Internal party democracy required.

[SECTION: 11] Article 18: Freedom of Trade and Profession — Every citizen has the right to enter any lawful profession or occupation, and to conduct any lawful trade or business, subject to qualifications prescribed by law.

[SECTION: 12] Article 19: Freedom of Speech — Freedom of speech and expression is guaranteed, subject to reasonable restrictions in the interests of: (a) glory of Islam; (b) security of Pakistan; (c) public order; (d) decency; (e) morality; (f) contempt of court; (g) defamation; (h) incitement to offence.

[SECTION: 13] Article 19A: Right to Information — Every citizen has the right to access information held by public bodies. Exercise of this right must not impinge on national security, personal privacy, or commercial confidentiality.

[SECTION: 14] Article 20: Freedom to Profess Religion — Subject to law, public order and morality: (a) every citizen has the right to profess, practice and propagate his religion; (b) every religious denomination has the right to establish and maintain religious institutions.

[SECTION: 15] Article 21: Protection against Taxation for Religious Purposes — No person shall be compelled to pay taxes for the propagation or maintenance of any religion other than his own.

[SECTION: 16] Article 22: Safeguards as to Educational Institutions — No person shall be required to receive religious instruction other than his own. Educational institutions maintained by religious communities may require religious instruction of their own faith.

[SECTION: 17] Article 23: Right to Hold Property — Every citizen has the right to acquire, hold and dispose of property subject to law. Property may be compulsorily acquired only for public purpose and with compensation.

[SECTION: 18] Article 24: Protection against Deprivation of Property — No person shall be compulsorily deprived of property save by law for a public purpose. Adequate compensation must be provided. Parliament may define 'public purpose' and 'compensation'.

[SECTION: 19] Article 25: Equality — All citizens are equal before law and entitled to equal protection of law. The State may make special provisions for the protection of women and children. Non-discrimination on grounds of sex alone.

[SECTION: 20] Article 25A: Right to Education — The State shall provide free and compulsory education to all children aged 5 to 16 years in such manner as determined by law.

[SECTION: 21] Article 26: Access to Public Places — No citizen shall be denied access to public places (hotels, restaurants, parks, entertainment venues) on grounds of race, religion, caste, sex, residence or place of birth.

[SECTION: 22] Article 27: Safeguard against Discrimination in Service — No citizen shall be discriminated against for public employment on grounds of race, religion, caste, sex, residence or place of birth.

[SECTION: 23] Article 28: Maintenance of Culture — Any section of citizens with distinct language, script or culture has the right to preserve and promote it. Admission to public educational institutions cannot be denied on linguistic grounds.

[SECTION: 24] Right to Remedy — The right to invoke writ jurisdiction under Article 199 (High Court) and Article 184 (Supreme Court) for enforcement of fundamental rights. This is the primary remedy for any violation of fundamental rights.

[SECTION: 25] Limitation on Fundamental Rights — Fundamental rights are not absolute. They are subject to reasonable restrictions imposed by law for the purposes specified. Rights may be suspended during a Proclamation of Emergency (Article 233).

[SECTION: 26] Non-discrimination and Minority Rights — Article 36: Protection of minorities. The State shall protect the legitimate rights and interests of minorities, including their due representation in Federal and Provincial services.
