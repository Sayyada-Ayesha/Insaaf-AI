# Customs Law of Pakistan — Customs Act, 1969
# Coverage: import/export framework, duty, clearance, offences, adjudication and appeals.
# Sources: Customs Act, 1969 (Act IV of 1969) as amended; FBR customs regime.
# Coverage level per section: PARTIAL (operative framework) / SUMMARY (structures).

[SECTION: CUSTOMS_READING_NOTE]
The Customs Act, 1969 is the principal federal customs statute, administered by the Federal Board
of Revenue (FBR) through its customs collectorates. Import and export of goods through sea, air
and land customs stations is regulated under the Act, SRO notifications and the Pakistan Customs
Tariff. Always check the latest SROs and Finance Act changes for rates and procedures.

[SECTION: CUSTOMS_IMPORT_FRAMEWORK]
LAW: Customs Act, 1969, Chapters on import. COVERAGE: PARTIAL.
KEY PROVISIONS (close paraphrase of the operative framework):
- Goods may be imported only through approved customs routes/stations and must be declared in
  the prescribed manner (Goods Declaration — GD) for assessment of duty and taxes.
- Customs value is determined under the customs valuation rules (transaction value method
  preferred, alternative methods where transaction value unavailable).
- Duties: customs duty under the Pakistan Customs Tariff plus regulatory duties and other
  federal taxes collected at import (sales tax, withholding, advance taxes) as applicable.
- Clearance: an importer clears goods through a licensed customs agent or directly; supporting
  documents (invoice, packing list, bill of lading/airway bill, import permit where required,
  valuation rulings) must accompany the GD.
- Examination and assessment: goods may be examined and assessed; goods are released on payment
  of duty/taxes and compliance with regulatory requirements.
SOURCE: Customs Act, 1969. VERIFICATION: verified as to framework.

[SECTION: CUSTOMS_EXPORT_FRAMEWORK]
LAW: Customs Act, 1969, export provisions. COVERAGE: PARTIAL.
KEY PROVISIONS (close paraphrase):
- Export goods are declared through the export GD; export duty applies where levied; zero-rated
  sales tax and refund/drawback regimes apply to exports under the tax laws.
- Restricted and prohibited items require export permits/licenses (e.g., controlled items).
- Export proceeds repatriation requirements apply under foreign-exchange regulations.
SOURCE: Customs Act, 1969. VERIFICATION: verified as to framework.

[SECTION: CUSTOMS_OFFENCES]
LAW: Customs Act, 1969, offences and penalties provisions (as amended). COVERAGE: PARTIAL.
KEY PROVISIONS (summary of the offence framework):
- Smuggling: importing/exporting goods in breach of prohibitions, or evading duty through
  concealment, false declaration or use of unauthorized routes, is punishable with penalties
  including confiscation of goods and conveyance, fines and imprisonment for serious cases.
- Misdeclaration of value, quantity or description attracts penalties and recovery of evaded
  duty with fines.
- Offences are adjudicated by customs officers (adjudication hierarchy) and, for scheduled
  offences, by the Customs Courts (Special Judge Customs) exercising jurisdiction under the
  Customs Act read with the relevant procedure.
SOURCE: Customs Act, 1969 (as amended). VERIFICATION: verified as to the framework (exact fine
and imprisonment ranges change with Finance Acts; check the current text).

[SECTION: CUSTOMS_APPEALS]
LAW: Customs Act, 1969, appeal provisions. COVERAGE: PARTIAL.
APPEAL CHAIN (summary): adjudication order -> appeal to Collector (Appeals) -> Customs Appellate
Tribunal -> reference/appeal to High Court on questions of law -> Supreme Court.
TIME LIMITS: appeals must be filed within the statutory periods from communication of the order
(check the current text for exact days).
SOURCE: Customs Act, 1969. VERIFICATION: verified as to the chain.

[SECTION: CUSTOMS_PERSONAL_BAGGAGE]
LAW: baggage rules under the Customs Act, 1969. COVERAGE: SUMMARY.
SUBSTANCE: passengers arriving in Pakistan may bring personal baggage within duty-free allowances
notified by FBR; goods beyond allowances or in commercial quantity are dutiable; currency and
valuables declaration rules apply on entry/exit (foreign-exchange regulations).
SOURCE: FBR baggage notifications. VERIFICATION: metadata-verified (allowances change by
notification).

[SECTION: CUSTOMS_AGENTS]
LAW: customs agents licensing under the Customs Act, 1969 and rules. COVERAGE: SUMMARY.
SUBSTANCE: persons clearing goods on behalf of importers/exporters must be licensed customs
agents (clearing agents) under the Customs Rules; agents act on behalf of principals and are
liable for compliance; licensing is by the collectorate.
SOURCE: Customs Rules. VERIFICATION: verified as to the licensing framework.
